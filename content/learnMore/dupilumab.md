## What is Dupilumab?

Dupilumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that blocks interleukin-4 (IL-4) receptor alpha, inhibiting signaling of both IL-4 and IL-13—key type 2 cytokines driving inflammation in atopic dermatitis, asthma, and chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps. By interrupting this pathway, dupilumab reduces IgE production, eosinophilic inflammation, and barrier dysfunction.

Administered subcutaneously every two weeks (loading dose on initiation for some indications). Unlike systemic immunosuppressants, it does not broadly suppress cell-mediated immunity, though conjunctivitis and injection-site reactions are common.

It represents biologic targeted therapy for type 2 inflammatory diseases rather than broad corticosteroid immunosuppression.

## Primary Uses

- Moderate to severe atopic dermatitis inadequately controlled by topicals
- Moderate to severe asthma with eosinophilic phenotype or oral corticosteroid dependence (add-on)
- Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (add-on intranasal therapy)
- Eosinophilic esophagitis (approved indication per current labeling)
- Prurigo nodularis (selected indications)

Prescribed by dermatology, allergy/immunology, or pulmonology specialists after step therapy.

## Common Side Effects

- Injection-site reactions (erythema, swelling, pain)
- Conjunctivitis and keratitis (eye examination if symptomatic)
- Oral herpes simplex reactivation (reported)
- Eosinophilia (usually transient)
- Arthralgia (rare)
- **Warnings:** Not for acute bronchospasm or status asthmaticus; do not discontinue systemic corticosteroids abruptly; caution with live vaccines; monitor for hypersensitivity; helminth infection risk theoretical in endemic areas.
