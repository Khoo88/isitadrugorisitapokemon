## What is Sitagliptin?

Sitagliptin is a dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) inhibitor that prevents degradation of endogenous incretin hormones GLP-1 and GIP, prolonging their action to increase glucose-dependent insulin secretion and suppress glucagon. It is weight-neutral and has low hypoglycemia risk as monotherapy.

Renal elimination requires dose reduction as estimated GFR declines. Unlike GLP-1 agonists, DPP-4 inhibitors do not cause significant weight loss or gastrointestinal effects in most patients.

Joint pain and bullous pemphigoid have been reported as class-associated rare adverse events. Pancreatitis risk is debated and likely very low.

## Primary Uses

- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (monotherapy or combination with metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin, etc.)
- Fixed-dose combinations with metformin (Janumet)

Used when oral therapy without injection is preferred and GLP-1 agonist is not chosen.

## Common Side Effects

- Upper respiratory tract infection, nasopharyngitis
- Headache
- Hypoglycemia (when combined with sulfonylureas or insulin)
- Rare: pancreatitis, severe joint pain, bullous pemphigoid
- **Warnings:** Dose adjust in renal impairment; discontinue if bullous pemphigoid suspected; heart failure hospitalization signal was primarily with saxagliptin, not established for sitagliptin.
