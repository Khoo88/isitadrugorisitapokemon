## What is Hydroxyzine?

Hydroxyzine is a first-generation antihistamine (H1 antagonist) with sedative, anxiolytic, and anticholinergic properties. It crosses the blood-brain barrier, producing histamine blockade centrally and peripherally for pruritus and allergic symptoms, and sedation for anxiety and preoperative use.

Active metabolite cetirizine contributes to antihistamine effect. It is not a benzodiazepine and is not a controlled substance in the United States, though sedation and cognitive impairment remain concerns, especially in elderly patients (Beers Criteria).

Metabolism via CYP3A4; QT prolongation reported at high doses.

## Primary Uses

- Pruritus and urticaria
- Generalized anxiety disorder (short-term, where non-controlled sedating option is desired)
- Premedication sedation before procedures
- Nausea and vomiting (adjunct)
- Alcohol withdrawal adjunct (selected protocols—not first-line)

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, drowsiness
- Dry mouth, blurred vision (anticholinergic)
- Confusion, delirium (elderly)
- Dizziness
- Headache
- QT prolongation at high doses
- **Warnings:** Avoid driving; caution with other CNS depressants; not preferred in elderly for chronic anxiety or insomnia; contraindicated in prolonged QT and early pregnancy per some labeling (risk-benefit in pregnancy varies).
