## What is Ribavirin?

Ribavirin is a synthetic nucleoside analog with broad antiviral activity through multiple mechanisms, including inhibition of inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase, altering guanine nucleotide pools and impairing viral RNA synthesis. It is teratogenic and embryotoxic—strict contraception is mandatory during and after therapy for both sexes.

Oral, inhaled, and intravenous formulations exist. Inhaled ribavirin treats severe RSV in selected hospitalized infants; oral/IV combinations with direct-acting antivirals historically treated hepatitis C before modern DAA regimens.

Hemolytic anemia is the most common dose-limiting toxicity due to accumulation in erythrocytes.

## Primary Uses

- Respiratory syncytial virus (aerosolized, severe infection in high-risk infants—specialist use)
- Hepatitis C (with direct-acting antivirals in selected genotypes/regimens—largely supplanted)
- Viral hemorrhagic fevers (e.g., Lassa, some COVID-19 investigational protocols)
- HCV in pregnancy is contraindicated; ribavirin contraindicated in pregnancy

Modern HCV therapy rarely requires ribavirin except in difficult genotypes or retreatment.

## Common Side Effects

- Hemolytic anemia (dose-dependent)
- Fatigue, irritability
- Nausea, cough (inhaled)
- Teratogenicity (absolute contraindication in pregnancy)
- Rash, pruritus
- **Warnings:** Two forms of contraception during therapy and 6 months after for men and women; monitor CBC weekly; teratogenic even with short exposure; pulmonary function decline with inhaled use in health care workers (occupational exposure precautions).
