## What is Hydroxychloroquine?

Hydroxychloroquine is an aminoquinoline antimalarial and immunomodulatory agent that accumulates in lysosomes, raising pH and interfering with antigen processing, toll-like receptor signaling, and cytokine production. Exact mechanisms in lupus and rheumatoid arthritis involve multiple pathways beyond antimalarial activity.

It is less retinotoxic than chloroquine at equivalent anti-inflammatory doses but still requires baseline and periodic ophthalmologic screening for maculopathy (optical coherence tomography and visual fields per current standards).

Long half-life means weeks to steady state and prolonged effects after discontinuation. It may prolong QT interval, relevant with other QT-prolonging drugs.

## Primary Uses

- Systemic lupus erythematosus (all patients without contraindication—reduces flares and mortality)
- Rheumatoid arthritis (disease-modifying adjunct)
- Sjögren syndrome, antiphospholipid syndrome (adjunct)
- Malaria prophylaxis and treatment (specific regions and protocols)
- Q fever, porphyria cutanea tarda (specialist indications)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, diarrhea, abdominal cramps (often improve with time)
- Rash, pruritus
- Retinal toxicity (irreversible maculopathy with prolonged high dose or risk factors)
- Cardiomyopathy (rare with chronic use)
- Myopathy, neuropathy (rare)
- **Warnings:** Baseline and annual eye exams after 5 years or earlier with risk factors; G6PD testing in high-risk populations for hemolysis; QT prolongation; do not exceed recommended daily weight-based dose; seizure threshold lowered in overdose.
