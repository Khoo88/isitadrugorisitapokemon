## What is Ibuprofen?

Ibuprofen is a nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that reversibly inhibits cyclooxygenase-1 and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX), reducing prostaglandin synthesis from arachidonic acid. Decreased prostaglandins mediate anti-inflammatory, analgesic, and antipyretic effects and account for gastrointestinal and renal adverse effects.

It is available over the counter and by prescription at higher doses. Platelet function inhibition is reversible compared with aspirin. All NSAIDs increase cardiovascular thrombotic risk with chronic high-dose use, though ibuprofen’s risk profile differs from naproxen and celecoxib in some analyses.

Renal prostaglandin reduction can precipitate acute kidney injury in volume-depleted patients or those with heart failure.

## Primary Uses

- Mild to moderate pain (headache, dysmenorrhea, musculoskeletal pain, dental pain)
- Fever reduction
- Inflammatory arthritis and soft tissue inflammation (prescription doses)
- Dysmenorrhea, migraine (acute, per guidelines)
- Patent ductus arteriosus closure in premature neonates (specialized neonatal dosing)

## Common Side Effects

- Dyspepsia, nausea, abdominal pain
- Gastrointestinal ulceration and bleeding
- Renal impairment, hyperkalemia, edema
- Elevated blood pressure
- Rash
- Cardiovascular thrombotic events (chronic use)
- **Warnings:** Avoid or minimize in renal failure, active GI bleed, high cardiovascular risk when alternatives exist; interaction with aspirin (competition for COX-1); use lowest effective dose and duration; caution with anticoagulants.
