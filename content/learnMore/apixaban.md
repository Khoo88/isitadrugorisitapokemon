## What is Apixaban?

Apixaban is a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) that selectively and reversibly inhibits factor Xa (FXa), blocking the conversion of prothrombin to thrombin in the coagulation cascade. Unlike warfarin, it does not require routine international normalized ratio (INR) monitoring and has fewer food and drug interactions related to CYP450 and vitamin K metabolism.

It is partially metabolized by CYP3A4 and is a substrate of P-glycoprotein; strong inhibitors or inducers of these pathways alter exposure. Approximately 25% renal elimination of unchanged drug makes dose reduction necessary in advanced chronic kidney disease for some indications.

Reversal agents (andexanet alfa) are available for life-threatening bleeding in apixaban-treated patients, though clinical use follows institutional protocols.

## Primary Uses

- Stroke and systemic embolism prevention in nonvalvular atrial fibrillation
- Treatment and secondary prevention of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and pulmonary embolism (PE)
- Postoperative thromboprophylaxis after hip or knee replacement surgery

Selection versus warfarin depends on renal function, drug interactions, patient preference, and cost. It is not indicated for mechanical heart valves or moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis.

## Common Side Effects

- Bleeding (major and minor—including gastrointestinal and intracranial)
- Bruising
- Nausea (less common than with some DOACs)
- Anemia related to bleeding
- **Warnings:** Discontinuation without adequate alternative anticoagulation increases thrombotic risk; spinal/epidural hematoma risk with neuraxial procedures; dose adjustment in age, weight, and renal criteria per labeling; caution with dual antiplatelet therapy (increased bleeding).
