## What is Metoprolol?

Metoprolol is a beta-1 selective adrenergic blocker (beta-blocker) that reduces heart rate, myocardial contractility, and renin release, lowering blood pressure and myocardial oxygen demand. Tartrate is immediate-release (twice daily); succinate is extended-release (once daily) for heart failure and hypertension.

Evidence-based mortality benefit in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and post-myocardial infarction applies to sustained-release metoprolol succinate in landmark trials. Beta-1 selectivity is relative at high doses, allowing some beta-2 blockade.

Metabolism via CYP2D6; genetic poor metabolizers may have higher levels. Abrupt withdrawal can cause rebound tachycardia and ischemia.

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (succinate, guideline-directed)
- Post-myocardial infarction (secondary prevention)
- Angina pectoris
- Supraventricular tachycardia rate control (acute IV tartrate)
- Migraine prophylaxis (off-label)
- Essential tremor (off-label)

## Common Side Effects

- Bradycardia, hypotension
- Fatigue, dizziness
- Cold extremities
- Bronchospasm (less than nonselective agents, caution in asthma)
- Sexual dysfunction
- Masking of hypoglycemia symptoms in diabetes
- **Warnings:** Do not stop abruptly in ischemic heart disease; contraindicated in decompensated heart failure acutely, high-grade AV block without pacemaker, and severe bradycardia; may worsen Raynaud phenomenon.
