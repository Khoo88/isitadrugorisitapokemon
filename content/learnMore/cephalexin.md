## What is Cephalexin?

Cephalexin is a first-generation cephalosporin antibiotic that inhibits peptidoglycan synthesis in bacterial cell walls. It is active primarily against gram-positive cocci, including Streptococcus and methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus, with limited gram-negative coverage compared with later cephalosporins.

Oral absorption is good on an empty or full stomach. Renal excretion of unchanged drug necessitates dose adjustment in renal impairment. It is commonly used for uncomplicated skin and urinary infections when pathogens are likely susceptible.

Cephalosporin cross-reactivity with penicillin allergy is lower than once believed for non-severe reactions, but assessment is individualized.

## Primary Uses

- Uncomplicated skin and soft tissue infections (cellulitis, impetigo)
- Streptococcal pharyngitis (alternative when penicillin unsuitable)
- Uncomplicated urinary tract infections (cystitis, when local resistance patterns support)
- Bone and joint infections (mild, outpatient, per culture)
- Surgical prophylaxis (selected dental and minor procedures)

Not reliable for atypical pneumonia, Pseudomonas, or MRSA without susceptibility confirmation.

## Common Side Effects

- Diarrhea, nausea, abdominal pain
- Rash (maculopapular)
- Allergic reactions (urticaria, anaphylaxis—rare)
- Clostridioides difficile-associated colitis
- Elevated liver enzymes (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Verify penicillin/cephalosporin allergy history; adjust dose in renal dysfunction; complete prescribed course; ineffective against beta-lactamase–producing organisms without combination therapy.
