## What is Digoxin?

Digoxin is a cardiac glycoside that inhibits the sodium-potassium ATPase pump in myocardial cells, increasing intracellular sodium and indirectly raising intracellular calcium, enhancing contractility (positive inotropy). It also increases vagal tone, slowing atrioventricular nodal conduction—useful for rate control in atrial fibrillation.

Narrow therapeutic index: subtherapeutic levels may fail to control symptoms; supratherapeutic levels cause arrhythmias and toxicity. Renal excretion predominates; hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and hypothyroidism increase toxicity risk. Many drugs alter digoxin levels (amiodarone, verapamil, clarithromycin).

Serum concentration monitoring and clinical assessment guide dosing in heart failure and atrial fibrillation.

## Primary Uses

- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (symptom relief and reduced hospitalization in selected patients on guideline-directed therapy)
- Rate control in atrial fibrillation (when beta-blockers or calcium channel blockers insufficient or contraindicated)

Not first-line for either indication in modern guidelines but remains valuable in specific patients.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, vomiting, anorexia (early toxicity)
- Visual disturbances (yellow-green halos)
- Arrhythmias (ventricular ectopy, bradycardia, heart block)
- Confusion, fatigue
- **Warnings:** Toxicity precipitated by electrolyte abnormalities and drug interactions; treat life-threatening toxicity with digoxin-specific antibody fragments (Fab); use weight- and renal-based dosing; avoid in certain pre-excitation syndromes.
