## What is Amiodarone?

Amiodarone is a class III antiarrhythmic agent with complex electrophysiologic properties: it blocks potassium channels prolonging repolarization, and also exhibits class I (sodium channel), class II (β-blockade), and class IV (calcium channel) effects. Its iodinated benzofuran structure contributes to very long tissue half-life (weeks to months) and extensive organ accumulation.

The drug prolongs action potential duration and refractory period in atrial and ventricular myocardium, suppressing reentrant arrhythmias and controlling ventricular rate in atrial fibrillation. Hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4 produces desethylamiodarone, an active metabolite. Numerous drug interactions occur through CYP inhibition and P-glycoprotein effects.

Baseline and serial monitoring of pulmonary, thyroid, hepatic, and ocular function is mandatory due to multisystem toxicity risk with chronic therapy.

## Primary Uses

- Life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias (recurrent ventricular fibrillation or hemodynamically unstable ventricular tachycardia)
- Atrial fibrillation rate and rhythm control when other agents fail or are contraindicated
- Post-cardiac arrest rhythm management (protocol-dependent)
- Supraventricular tachycardias in selected refractory cases

Often reserved as second- or third-line due to toxicity profile; dronedarone is a related agent with reduced iodine load but different indications and contraindications.

## Common Side Effects

- Pulmonary toxicity (interstitial pneumonitis, fibrosis—potentially fatal)
- Thyroid dysfunction (hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism from iodine load)
- Hepatotoxicity, elevated transaminases
- Bradycardia, heart block, QT prolongation and torsades de pointes (less common than class IA/IC agents but possible)
- Corneal microdeposits (often asymptomatic), optic neuropathy (rare)
- Photosensitivity, blue-gray skin discoloration
- Peripheral neuropathy, tremor, ataxia
- Nausea, constipation
- **Warnings:** Requires structured monitoring; contraindicated in significant bradycardia, second- or third-degree block without pacemaker, and pregnancy; extensive drug interaction profile.
