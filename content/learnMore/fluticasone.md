## What is Fluticasone?

Fluticasone is a synthetic inhaled, intranasal, and topical corticosteroid with high glucocorticoid receptor affinity and extensive first-pass metabolism when swallowed from inhaled or nasal routes, limiting systemic effects at recommended doses. It suppresses local inflammation by inhibiting phospholipase A2, cytokines, and inflammatory cell recruitment.

Formulations include metered-dose inhalers, dry powder inhalers, nasal sprays, and topical creams for dermatitis. Combination products pair fluticasone with long-acting beta-agonists (salmeterol, vilanterol) or anticholinergics (umeclidinium) for asthma and COPD.

Prolonged high-dose use can cause adrenal suppression, growth effects in children, and ocular effects; rinse mouth after inhalation to reduce oral candidiasis.

## Primary Uses

- Persistent asthma maintenance therapy (inhaled)
- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease maintenance (combination inhalers)
- Allergic and nonallergic rhinitis (intranasal)
- Atopic dermatitis and inflammatory skin conditions (topical)
- Eosinophilic esophagitis (swallowed inhaled preparation, per gastroenterology protocols)

## Common Side Effects

**Inhaled:**
- Oral candidiasis (thrush)
- Hoarseness, dysphonia
- Cough

**Intranasal:**
- Epistaxis, nasal irritation

**Topical:**
- Skin atrophy, striae with prolonged use

**Systemic (high dose/long term):**
- Adrenal suppression, cataracts, glaucoma risk
- **Warnings:** Do not use for acute bronchospasm; taper systemic steroids carefully when switching; live vaccines caution on immunosuppressive doses.
