## What is Pantoprazole?

Pantoprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) that irreversibly inhibits the gastric H+/K+-ATPase proton pump on parietal cells, suppressing gastric acid secretion regardless of the stimulatory pathway. It is a benzimidazole prodrug activated in the acidic canalicular environment.

Once-daily dosing provides sustained acid suppression for 24 hours until new pump molecules are synthesized. Metabolism is primarily hepatic via CYP2C19; fewer drug interactions with clopidogrel were proposed for pantoprazole versus omeprazole in some studies, though clinical significance remains debated.

Long-term PPI use is associated with hypomagnesemia, vitamin B12 deficiency, C. difficile infection risk, and possible kidney disease associations; periodic reassessment of indication is recommended.

## Primary Uses

- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
- Erosive esophagitis healing and maintenance
- Peptic ulcer disease
- Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
- Stress ulcer prophylaxis in critical illness (IV formulation, institutional protocols)
- NSAID-associated gastroprotection

Available oral and intravenous formulations for inpatient use.

## Common Side Effects

- Headache
- Diarrhea, abdominal pain, nausea
- Flatulence
- Long-term: hypomagnesemia, B12 deficiency, fracture risk (debated), acute interstitial nephritis (rare)
- **Warnings:** May reduce absorption of drugs requiring acid (iron, ketoconazole); evaluate alarm symptoms before chronic use; C. difficile risk with prolonged therapy.
