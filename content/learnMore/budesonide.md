## What is Budesonide?

Budesonide is a synthetic corticosteroid with high topical glucocorticoid receptor affinity and extensive first-pass hepatic metabolism when administered orally for intestinal delivery. It binds intracellular glucocorticoid receptors, modulating gene transcription to reduce proinflammatory cytokines, eosinophils, and mucus production.

Formulations include inhaled (asthma, COPD maintenance), intranasal (allergic rhinitis), oral delayed-release (Crohn’s disease, ulcerative colitis), and rectal foam/enema. Systemic effects are lower than prednisone at equivalent anti-inflammatory local doses due to route and metabolism, though suppression can still occur at higher doses or prolonged use.

Inhaled budesonide is a cornerstone controller therapy in asthma; oral budesonide targets mucosal disease in inflammatory bowel disease with less systemic steroid exposure than conventional corticosteroids.

## Primary Uses

- Persistent asthma maintenance (inhaled, often with long-acting beta-agonist)
- Allergic and perennial rhinitis (intranasal)
- Mild to moderate Crohn’s disease (ileal/right colon)
- Ulcerative colitis (rectal and oral formulations per disease distribution)
- Eosinophilic esophagitis (swallowed inhaled preparation, off-label per guidelines)
- COPD maintenance (in combination inhalers)

## Common Side Effects

**Inhaled:**
- Oral candidiasis (thrush)
- Hoarseness, dysphonia
- Cough
- Systemic effects at high doses (adrenal suppression, bone density loss with chronic use)

**Intranasal:**
- Epistaxis, nasal irritation

**Oral/rectal:**
- Headache, nausea
- Adrenal suppression (less than prednisone but possible)
- Mood changes, hyperglycemia with prolonged systemic absorption
- **Warnings:** Rinse mouth after inhalation; taper systemic steroids carefully when switching; live vaccines contraindicated on immunosuppressive doses.
