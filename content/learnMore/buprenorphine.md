## What is Buprenorphine?

Buprenorphine is a partial mu-opioid receptor agonist and kappa-opioid receptor antagonist used in opioid use disorder and pain management. Partial agonism at mu receptors produces analgesia and reduces craving and withdrawal with a ceiling effect on respiratory depression at moderate doses, though overdose remains possible especially with polydrug use.

It has high receptor affinity and slow dissociation, which can precipitate withdrawal if started before other opioids have cleared. The transmucosal and long-acting injectable formulations support medication-assisted treatment (MAT) for opioid dependence.

Metabolism occurs via CYP3A4 to norbuprenorphine. Combination products with naloxone deter intravenous misuse of tablets (naloxone is inactive orally but blocks opioid effects if injected).

## Primary Uses

- Opioid use disorder maintenance treatment (sublingual film/tablet, buccal, long-acting injection)
- Chronic pain management (transdermal patch, buccal film—when continuous opioid analgesia is appropriate)
- Perioperative pain in opioid-tolerant patients (selected settings)

Prescribing for opioid use disorder requires waiver/training per jurisdiction (X-waiver requirements evolved in U.S. regulations).

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, constipation
- Headache, insomnia
- Sweating
- Withdrawal symptoms if started too early or with full agonist on board
- Respiratory depression (especially with benzodiazepines, alcohol, or full agonists)
- Hepatitis with intravenous misuse of tablets (rare)
- **Warnings:** Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies for some products; avoid unsupervised benzodiazepine co-use; precipitated withdrawal; neonatal opioid withdrawal in pregnancy; Schedule III controlled substance.
