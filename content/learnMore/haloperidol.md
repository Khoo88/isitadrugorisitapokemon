## What is Haloperidol?

Haloperidol is a high-potency typical (first-generation) antipsychotic that primarily antagonizes dopamine D2 receptors in mesolimbic pathways, reducing positive psychotic symptoms. It has strong extrapyramidal side effect liability and minimal anticholinergic or sedative properties compared with low-potency typicals like chlorpromazine.

Parenteral formulations allow rapid control of acute agitation and delirium in monitored settings. Metabolism via CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 produces active and inactive metabolites; depot injectable forms exist for maintenance schizophrenia treatment.

QT prolongation and neuroleptic malignant syndrome are class risks across antipsychotics.

## Primary Uses

- Schizophrenia (acute and maintenance, oral and depot)
- Acute agitation and psychosis (intramuscular, short-term)
- Tourette disorder (selected cases)
- Delirium in hospitalized patients (low-dose, short-term, per institutional protocols—non-pharmacologic measures preferred first)
- Severe nausea and vomiting (adjunct, limited)

## Common Side Effects

- Extrapyramidal symptoms (dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia with chronic use)
- Hyperprolactinemia (galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction)
- Sedation (less than low-potency agents)
- QT prolongation
- Neuroleptic malignant syndrome (rare, life-threatening)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in Lewy body dementia when possible (severe sensitivity); monitor QT in cardiac disease; elderly at risk for falls and mortality with antipsychotics in dementia-related psychosis (boxed warning for increased mortality).
