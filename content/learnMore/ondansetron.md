## What is Ondansetron?

Ondansetron is a selective serotonin 5-HT3 receptor antagonist that blocks serotonin at vagal afferents and chemoreceptor trigger zone, preventing nausea and vomiting from chemotherapy, radiation, surgery, and gastroenteritis (off-label). It has minimal dopamine antagonism, so extrapyramidal effects are uncommon.

Oral, orally disintegrating, and intravenous formulations exist. QT interval prolongation is a class effect at higher doses; FDA recommends caution in congenital long QT, electrolyte abnormalities, and concurrent QT-prolonging drugs.

It does not stimulate gastric motility (unlike metoclopramide) and is ineffective for gastroparesis-related delayed emptying.

## Primary Uses

- Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (moderate and highly emetogenic regimens)
- Radiation-induced nausea and vomiting
- Postoperative nausea and vomiting (PONV prophylaxis and treatment)
- Acute gastroenteritis in children and adults (off-label, common emergency practice)
- Hyperemesis gravidarum (selected cases after first-line measures, risk-benefit discussion)

## Common Side Effects

- Headache
- Constipation
- Diarrhea
- Dizziness
- QT prolongation (dose-dependent)
- Serotonin syndrome (rare, with other serotonergic drugs)
- **Warnings:** ECG monitoring in high-risk patients at IV doses; ODT phenylalanine in PKU patients; limited data favoring routine use in uncomplicated gastroenteritis in some guidelines due to cost and QT risk—still widely used.
