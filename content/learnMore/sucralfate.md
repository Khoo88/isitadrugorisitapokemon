## What is Sucralfate?

Sucralfate is a basic aluminum salt of sucrose sulfate that, in acidic gastric environments, polymerizes into a viscous gel that binds to positively charged proteins in ulcer craters and inflamed mucosa, forming a protective barrier against acid, pepsin, and bile. It also stimulates local prostaglandin production and binds bile salts.

Systemic absorption is minimal; most effects are local. It requires dosing on an empty stomach with separation from other medications (binds many drugs in gut lumen).

Use has declined with potent PPIs but remains useful for ulcer healing and stress gastropathy in selected patients.

## Primary Uses

- Duodenal ulcer healing and maintenance
- Gastric ulcer (adjunct)
- Stress-related mucosal damage prophylaxis (selected ICU protocols, though PPIs dominate)
- Gastroesophageal reflux (limited efficacy vs. PPIs)

## Common Side Effects

- Constipation (most common)
- Dry mouth
- Aluminum load with long-term use (avoid in renal failure—aluminum toxicity, osteomalacia)
- Bezoar formation (rare, with altered GI motility)
- **Warnings:** Separate dosing from fluoroquinolones, levothyroxine, phenytoin, and other drugs by 2 hours; contraindicated in chronic renal failure due to aluminum accumulation; may lower phosphate.
