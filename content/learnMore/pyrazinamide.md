## What is Pyrazinamide?

Pyrazinamide is an antimycobacterial agent active against Mycobacterium tuberculosis in acidic environments within macrophages and caseous necrosis. Its exact mechanism is incompletely understood but involves pyrazinoic acid accumulation after conversion by pyrazinamidase, disrupting membrane energetics and fatty acid synthesis in susceptible bacilli.

It is bactericidal in the initial intensive phase of tuberculosis treatment alongside isoniazid, rifampin, and ethambutol, allowing shortening of total therapy duration. Hepatotoxicity is common enough to require monitoring; hyperuricemia occurs through urate transporter effects and may cause arthralgia.

Dosing is weight-based; hepatic and renal function guide therapy in comorbid patients.

## Primary Uses

- Active pulmonary and extrapulmonary tuberculosis (initial 2-month intensive phase of standard regimens)
- Mycobacterium tuberculosis in combination only—never monotherapy

Part of WHO and CDC first-line TB protocols globally.

## Common Side Effects

- Hepatotoxicity (elevated transaminases, jaundice—monitor closely)
- Hyperuricemia, gout-like arthralgia
- Nausea, anorexia
- Rash, pruritus
- Photosensitivity (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Monthly liver function monitoring; discontinue if significant hepatitis; contraindicated in acute gout and severe hepatic disease; pregnancy use requires specialist risk-benefit; ethionamide cross-reactivity considerations in resistance regimens.
