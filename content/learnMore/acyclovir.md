## What is Acyclovir?

Acyclovir is a synthetic nucleoside analog antiviral agent active against herpes simplex virus (HSV) types 1 and 2, varicella-zoster virus (VZV), and to a lesser extent Epstein-Barr virus. It requires phosphorylation by viral thymidine kinase to acyclovir monophosphate, then cellular kinases convert it to the active triphosphate form, which competitively inhibits viral DNA polymerase and causes chain termination of viral DNA synthesis.

Because host cell kinases phosphorylate acyclovir far less efficiently than viral thymidine kinase, the drug exhibits selective toxicity toward infected cells. Oral bioavailability is modest (approximately 15–30%); intravenous formulations achieve higher systemic exposure for severe infections. Renal elimination of unchanged drug predominates; dose adjustment is required in renal impairment.

Resistance can emerge with deficient viral thymidine kinase or altered DNA polymerase, particularly in immunocompromised patients receiving prolonged therapy.

## Primary Uses

- Treatment of initial and recurrent genital herpes (HSV-2)
- Treatment of mucocutaneous HSV infections in immunocompromised patients
- Herpes labialis (cold sores) when initiated early
- Varicella (chickenpox) and herpes zoster (shingles) in selected patients
- Prophylaxis of HSV reactivation in transplant and other immunocompromised populations
- Neonatal HSV infection (intravenous therapy)

Valacyclovir and famciclovir are prodrugs often preferred for oral convenience, but acyclovir remains a standard, cost-effective option across inpatient and outpatient settings.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain
- Headache, dizziness, fatigue
- Rash (including rare hypersensitivity)
- Renal dysfunction, especially with high-dose intravenous therapy or dehydration (crystalline nephropathy risk—adequate hydration is recommended)
- Neurotoxicity at high levels (confusion, tremor, seizures)—more likely in renal failure or elderly patients
- Mild elevations in liver enzymes (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Adjust dose in renal impairment; maintain hydration with IV use; caution in pregnancy (generally used when benefit outweighs risk).
