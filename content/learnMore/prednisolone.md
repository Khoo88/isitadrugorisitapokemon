## What is Prednisolone?

Prednisolone is a synthetic glucocorticoid that binds intracellular glucocorticoid receptors, regulating gene transcription to suppress proinflammatory cytokines, prostaglandins, and immune cell migration. It has greater mineralocorticoid activity than dexamethasone but less than hydrocortisone.

Oral bioavailability is reliable without hepatic activation (unlike prednisone, which requires conversion to prednisolone). It is used in asthma exacerbations, allergic conditions, autoimmune flares, and as replacement when adrenal suppression exists.

Prolonged use causes adrenal axis suppression, osteoporosis, hyperglycemia, infection risk, and cushingoid features; tapering is required after sustained therapy.

## Primary Uses

- Asthma and COPD exacerbations (oral burst)
- Allergic and inflammatory conditions (dermatitis, rhinitis, angioedema adjunct)
- Rheumatologic disease flares (RA, lupus, vasculitis—bridge therapy)
- Inflammatory bowel disease flares
- Adrenal insufficiency replacement (with mineralocorticoid as needed)
- Pediatric croup (oral or dexamethasone preferred in many protocols)

## Common Side Effects

- Hyperglycemia, weight gain, moon facies
- Insomnia, mood changes, psychosis
- Hypertension, fluid retention
- Osteoporosis, avascular necrosis (chronic use)
- Increased infection risk
- Adrenal suppression on withdrawal
- **Warnings:** Taper after prolonged use; avoid live vaccines during immunosuppressive doses; gastric protection considered with NSAIDs; growth suppression in children with chronic use.
