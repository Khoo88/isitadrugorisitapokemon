## What is Brimonidine?

Brimonidine is a selective alpha-2 adrenergic receptor agonist used primarily as topical ophthalmic solution to lower intraocular pressure (IOP). Activation of alpha-2 receptors in the ciliary body decreases aqueous humor production and increases uveoscleral outflow.

A topical dermatologic formulation exists for facial erythema of rosacea, where vasoconstriction reduces flushing. Systemic absorption from eye drops can occur, particularly in pediatric patients, producing central nervous system and cardiovascular effects.

It is structurally related to clonidine but formulated for local ocular effect at standard ophthalmic concentrations.

## Primary Uses

- Open-angle glaucoma
- Ocular hypertension
- Topical gel for persistent facial erythema of rosacea (dermatologic indication)

Often used as adjunct or alternative when prostaglandin analogs or beta-blockers are insufficient or not tolerated.

## Common Side Effects

**Ophthalmic:**
- Ocular hyperemia, burning, stinging
- Blurred vision, foreign body sensation
- Dry mouth (systemic absorption)
- Fatigue, drowsiness (especially in children—use caution)
- Allergic conjunctivitis with long-term use (follicular conjunctivitis)

**Dermatologic:**
- Local irritation, worsening erythema on discontinuation (rebound flushing)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in neonates and young children for glaucoma (risk of apnea, bradycardia); use cautiously in cardiovascular disease; may interact with MAO inhibitors and CNS depressants.
