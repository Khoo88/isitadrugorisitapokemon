## What is Escitalopram?

Escitalopram is the S-enantiomer of citalopram and a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI). It blocks the serotonin transporter (SERT), increasing synaptic serotonin available for postsynaptic receptor signaling. Allosteric modulation of the transporter may contribute to its pharmacologic profile.

Metabolized primarily by CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 to inactive metabolites. It is among the most selective SSRIs for serotonin versus norepinephrine reuptake at standard doses, with generally favorable tolerability in comparative trials.

Onset of antidepressant effect requires weeks; anxiolytic benefit may appear earlier in some patients.

## Primary Uses

- Major depressive disorder
- Generalized anxiety disorder
- Panic disorder
- Social anxiety disorder (social phobia)

First-line agent in many treatment guidelines for depression and anxiety disorders; available as generic with once-daily dosing.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, diarrhea, dry mouth
- Sexual dysfunction (decreased libido, delayed orgasm, erectile dysfunction)
- Insomnia or somnolence
- Headache, fatigue
- Sweating
- Serotonin syndrome (with MAO inhibitors or other serotonergic drugs)
- QT prolongation at supratherapeutic doses (less concern than racemic citalopram at standard doses)
- **Warnings:** Antidepressant suicidality boxed warning in young adults; taper when discontinuing; hyponatremia in elderly; bleeding risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants; do not use with MAOIs within 14 days.
