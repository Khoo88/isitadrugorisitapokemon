## What is Simvastatin?

Simvastatin is a lipophilic HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) derived from fungal fermentation products, metabolized by CYP3A4 to active beta-hydroxy acid forms that inhibit hepatic cholesterol synthesis. LDL receptor upregulation lowers circulating LDL cholesterol.

FDA limits maximum dose to 40 mg daily for most patients due to myopathy risk, especially with interacting drugs (amiodarone, amlodipine, diltiazem, verapamil, fibrates, azole antifungals). Evening dosing aligns with peak cholesterol synthesis overnight.

Extensive drug interactions via CYP3A4 distinguish it from pravastatin and rosuvastatin.

## Primary Uses

- Hyperlipidemia and cardiovascular risk reduction
- Secondary prevention post-MI or stroke
- Homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (high-dose specialist use)

Often replaced by more potent statins when aggressive LDL lowering is needed.

## Common Side Effects

- Myalgia, rhabdomyolysis (dose and interaction dependent)
- Elevated hepatic transaminases
- Headache, gastrointestinal upset
- **Warnings:** 80 mg dose restricted; avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors; grapefruit juice increases levels; pregnancy contraindicated; gemfibrozil combination increases myopathy risk substantially.
