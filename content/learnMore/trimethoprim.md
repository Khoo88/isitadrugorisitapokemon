## What is Trimethoprim?

Trimethoprim inhibits bacterial dihydrofolate reductase, blocking folate synthesis required for DNA and RNA production. It is bacteriostatic alone and often combined with sulfamethoxazole (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, TMP-SMX), which inhibits dihydropteroate synthase, producing sequential blockade of folate metabolism (synergy).

TMP-SMX covers many gram-positive, gram-negative, and opportunistic organisms (Staphylococcus aureus including community MRSA, Pneumocystis jirovecii, Toxoplasma). Trimethoprim alone is used for uncomplicated UTI when local resistance is low.

Hyperkalemia can occur through amiloride-like effects on renal epithelial sodium channels. Stevens-Johnson syndrome risk with sulfa component.

## Primary Uses

- Uncomplicated urinary tract infection (trimethoprim alone in some regions)
- TMP-SMX: urinary tract infection, pyelonephritis, skin infections (MRSA), travelers’ diarrhea
- Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia prophylaxis and treatment (with HIV)
- Toxoplasmosis prophylaxis and treatment
- Acute exacerbations of chronic bronchitis (selected)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, rash (especially TMP-SMX)
- Hyperkalemia (trimethoprim, especially in renal impairment or with ACE inhibitors)
- Photosensitivity (TMP-SMX)
- Bone marrow suppression (folate deficiency, rare)
- Stevens-Johnson syndrome / toxic epidermal necrolysis (TMP-SMX)
- **Warnings:** Avoid TMP-SMX in sulfa allergy; adjust dose in renal failure; methotrexate interaction (pancytopenia); G6PD hemolysis risk; pregnancy—avoid near term (kernicterus risk with sulfa).
