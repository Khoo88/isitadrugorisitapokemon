## What is Clindamycin?

Clindamycin is a lincosamide antibiotic that binds the 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis. It is active against gram-positive cocci (including many community-acquired MRSA strains), anaerobes, and some protozoa; it lacks activity against gram-negative aerobes.

Excellent tissue penetration including bone and abscess cavities makes it valuable for skin, soft tissue, and anaerobic infections. It does not achieve meaningful cerebrospinal fluid levels for central nervous system infections.

Inhibition of bacterial protein synthesis can suppress toxin production in toxin-mediated infections (e.g., necrotizing fasciitis with streptococcal toxic shock, though penicillin remains core for streptococcus).

## Primary Uses

- Skin and soft tissue infections (purulent cellulitis, abscess—often after incision and drainage)
- Community-acquired MRSA infections (oral or IV, susceptibility permitting)
- Aspiration pneumonia and anaerobic pulmonary infections (combination regimens)
- Dental infections and odontogenic abscess
- Toxin-suppressing adjunct in invasive group A streptococcal disease (per protocol)
- Bacterial vaginosis (topical vaginal formulation)
- Acne (topical)

## Common Side Effects

- Diarrhea
- Clostridioides difficile colitis (higher risk than many antibiotics)
- Nausea, abdominal pain
- Rash, drug reaction with eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS—rare)
- Esophagitis (oral capsules without adequate water)
- **Warnings:** Reserve when indicated due to C. difficile risk; monitor for severe colitis; caution in liver disease; neuromuscular blockade potentiation with paralytics.
