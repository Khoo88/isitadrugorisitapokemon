## What is Metoclopramide?

Metoclopramide is a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist that increases acetylcholine release in the gastrointestinal tract, enhancing gastric emptying and tightening the lower esophageal sphincter. It also has central antiemetic effects at the chemoreceptor trigger zone.

Because it crosses the blood-brain barrier, extrapyramidal side effects (dystonia, tardive dyskinesia) are significant, especially in young patients and with prolonged use. FDA recommends limiting duration to 12 weeks except in cases where benefit outweighs risk.

IV formulations are used for gastroparesis and postoperative nausea; oral use for reflux and diabetic gastroparesis is increasingly cautious given safer alternatives.

## Primary Uses

- Diabetic gastroparesis (short-term)
- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (refractory symptoms, short-term)
- Prevention and treatment of chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (with other agents)
- Postoperative nausea and vomiting (IV)
- Lactation stimulation (off-label—not recommended first-line due to CNS risks)

## Common Side Effects

- Drowsiness, fatigue
- Extrapyramidal reactions (acute dystonia, akathisia, parkinsonism)
- Tardive dyskinesia (potentially irreversible with chronic use)
- Diarrhea, restlessness
- Galactorrhea, hyperprolactinemia
- **Warnings:** Black box warning for tardive dyskinesia; avoid in Parkinson disease; limit duration; contraindicated with bowel obstruction; QT prolongation reported.
