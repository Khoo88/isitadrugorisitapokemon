## What is Bisacodyl?

Bisacodyl is a stimulant laxative that acts locally in the large intestine after hydrolysis to its active form. It stimulates colonic mucosa, increasing peristalsis and fluid and electrolyte secretion into the bowel lumen. Enteric coating delays release until the drug reaches the colon, reducing upper gastrointestinal irritation.

It is used for short-term relief of constipation and for bowel preparation before procedures when included in multi-agent regimens. Prolonged or excessive use can cause dependency on laxatives for bowel movements and electrolyte disturbances.

Unlike osmotic laxatives (polyethylene glycol), stimulant laxatives work more rapidly for acute relief but are not preferred for chronic daily management in most guidelines.

## Primary Uses

- Short-term treatment of occasional constipation
- Bowel evacuation before colonoscopy or surgery (as part of combination bowel prep protocols)
- Relief of constipation associated with opioid use (often with other agents in a bowel regimen)

Chronic constipation management typically prioritizes fiber, fluids, osmotic laxatives, and treatment of underlying causes.

## Common Side Effects

- Abdominal cramping, pain
- Diarrhea
- Nausea
- Rectal irritation (suppository formulation)
- Electrolyte imbalance with chronic overuse (hypokalemia)
- Melanosis coli with long-term stimulant laxative use
- **Warnings:** Avoid in acute abdominal conditions, obstruction, and severe dehydration; not for long-term daily use without medical supervision; may cause watery diarrhea with bowel prep doses.
