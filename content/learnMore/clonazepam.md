## What is Clonazepam?

Clonazepam is a long-acting benzodiazepine that enhances GABAergic inhibition at GABAA receptors, increasing chloride influx and neuronal hyperpolarization. It has anticonvulsant, anxiolytic, and muscle relaxant properties with a longer half-life than diazepam or lorazepam in many patients, allowing less frequent dosing.

Used for seizure disorders and panic disorder, it carries dependence and withdrawal risks with chronic use. Cognitive and psychomotor impairment can affect driving and fall risk in older adults.

Metabolized by CYP3A4 to inactive metabolites. It is a Schedule IV controlled substance.

## Primary Uses

- Panic disorder with or without agoraphobia
- Seizure disorders (absence, myoclonic, and certain epilepsies—as adjunct or monotherapy per neurology guidance)
- Akathisia and acute catatonia (short-term adjunct, specialist use)
- REM sleep behavior disorder (off-label, per sleep medicine guidelines)

Long-term benzodiazepine use for anxiety is generally discouraged in favor of SSRIs/SNRIs and psychotherapy.

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, fatigue, cognitive slowing
- Ataxia, impaired coordination
- Dependence, tolerance, withdrawal on discontinuation
- Depression exacerbation (paradoxical reactions possible)
- Respiratory depression with opioids or alcohol
- Salivation changes, behavioral changes in children
- **Warnings:** Boxed warning with concomitant opioids; avoid abrupt cessation; fall risk in elderly; pregnancy category risk (neonatal withdrawal); use lowest effective dose for shortest duration when prescribed for anxiety.
