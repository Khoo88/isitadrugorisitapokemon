## What is Alprazolam?

Alprazolam is a triazolobenzodiazepine that enhances the effect of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) at the GABAA receptor by increasing the frequency of chloride channel opening. This produces anxiolytic, sedative, muscle relaxant, and anticonvulsant effects at clinically used doses.

It is rapidly absorbed and has a relatively short half-life with active metabolites, contributing to quick onset and pronounced withdrawal potential if discontinued abruptly. Lipophilicity allows central nervous system penetration, accounting for potent anxiolytic and sedative properties compared with some other benzodiazepines.

Chronic use leads to tolerance, physical dependence, and cognitive effects. Alprazolam is classified as a Schedule IV controlled substance in the United States due to abuse and dependence risk.

## Primary Uses

- Generalized anxiety disorder (short-term therapy per guidelines; SSRIs/SNRIs preferred for long-term management)
- Panic disorder with or without agoraphobia
- Short-term adjunct for acute anxiety or insomnia in selected situations

Current psychiatric guidelines generally favor psychotherapy and antidepressants for chronic anxiety; benzodiazepines are reserved for time-limited use when other interventions are insufficient or during bridging periods.

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, drowsiness, fatigue
- Cognitive impairment, confusion (especially in elderly)
- Dizziness, impaired coordination
- Dependence and withdrawal symptoms on discontinuation (anxiety rebound, insomnia, seizures in severe cases)
- Respiratory depression when combined with opioids or alcohol
- Paradoxical agitation or disinhibition (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Boxed warning for concomitant opioid use (respiratory depression, death); avoid abrupt cessation; high misuse potential; fall risk in older adults; use lowest effective dose for shortest duration.
