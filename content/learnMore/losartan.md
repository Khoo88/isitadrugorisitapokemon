## What is Losartan?

Losartan is an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB) that selectively antagonizes the AT1 receptor, preventing angiotensin II–mediated vasoconstriction, aldosterone release, and cardiac remodeling. Unlike ACE inhibitors, ARBs do not inhibit kinin breakdown, so dry cough is uncommon.

Losartan is a prodrug; active metabolite EXP3174 provides long duration of action supporting once-daily dosing. It has uric acid–lowering properties unique among ARBs, occasionally useful in hypertensive patients with gout.

ARBs are alternatives when ACE inhibitors are not tolerated and share similar benefits in heart failure, post-MI, and diabetic nephropathy (though ACE inhibitors remain preferred in some guidelines for heart failure unless contraindicated).

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension
- Diabetic nephropathy with proteinuria (type 2 diabetes)
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (when ACE inhibitor not tolerated)
- Stroke prevention in left ventricular hypertrophy with hypertension (historical indication)
- Marfan syndrome aortic root protection (off-label, specialist use with beta-blockers)

## Common Side Effects

- Dizziness, hypotension
- Hyperkalemia
- Elevated creatinine (hemodynamic)
- Diarrhea, upper respiratory symptoms
- Rare angioedema (less than ACE inhibitors)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated in pregnancy; monitor potassium and renal function; avoid combined use with aliskiren in diabetes; fetal toxicity in second and third trimesters.
