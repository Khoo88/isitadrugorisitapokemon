## What is Meropenem?

Meropenem is a carbapenem beta-lactam antibiotic that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins with high affinity, causing cell lysis. It resists most beta-lactamases, including extended-spectrum beta-lactamases (ESBLs), and has broad gram-negative and gram-positive coverage (excluding methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus and Enterococcus faecium).

Parenteral administration only; used for serious hospital-acquired and health care–associated infections. Seizure risk is lower than imipenem at standard doses but still elevated in renal failure without dose adjustment.

Overuse promotes carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales (CRE); stewardship is essential.

## Primary Uses

- Complicated intra-abdominal infections
- Complicated urinary tract infections and pyelonephritis
- Hospital-acquired and ventilator-associated pneumonia
- Febrile neutropenia (empiric, combination regimens)
- Meningitis (including multidrug-resistant pathogens per susceptibility)
- Skin and soft tissue infections (severe)
- Sepsis with suspected resistant gram-negative organisms

Often combined with other agents for pseudomonal coverage or synergy.

## Common Side Effects

- Diarrhea, nausea
- Rash, drug fever
- Seizures (renal impairment, high dose)
- Clostridioides difficile colitis
- Thrombocytopenia, eosinophilia
- **Warnings:** Adjust dose in renal failure; assess penicillin allergy (cross-reactivity low for IgE but institutional protocols vary); reserve for serious infections; valproic acid levels reduced (major interaction).
