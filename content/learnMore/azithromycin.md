## What is Azithromycin?

Azithromycin is a macrolide antibiotic that binds the 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis. It is bacteriostatic at lower concentrations and bactericidal at higher concentrations against susceptible organisms. Its azalide structure provides extensive tissue penetration and a prolonged half-life, allowing short courses (e.g., 3–5 days) for many indications.

Acid stability permits oral absorption; hepatic metabolism yields inactive metabolites with biliary excretion contributing to elimination. Activity covers atypical pathogens (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Legionella) and some gram-positive organisms, but not typical for methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus or many enteric gram-negatives.

QT interval prolongation is a class-related electrophysiologic concern, especially with concomitant arrhythmogenic drugs or baseline cardiac disease.

## Primary Uses

- Community-acquired pneumonia (outpatient, atypical coverage)
- Acute bacterial exacerbations of COPD (selected regimens)
- Pharyngitis/tonsillitis (penicillin allergy, streptococcal)
- Uncomplicated skin and soft tissue infections
- Chlamydia trachomatis urethritis/cervicitis
- Mycobacterium avium complex prophylaxis and treatment (multi-drug regimens)
- Acute otitis media (alternative when first-line unsuitable)

Prescribing should follow local resistance patterns; macrolide resistance in Streptococcus pneumoniae limits empiric use in some regions.

## Common Side Effects

- Diarrhea, nausea, abdominal pain
- Dysgeusia (abnormal taste)
- QT prolongation and arrhythmia risk (torsades de pointes—rare)
- Hepatotoxicity (cholestatic, uncommon)
- Allergic reactions (rash, rare anaphylaxis)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in patients with congenital long QT, bradyarrhythmias, or concurrent QT-prolonging drugs when possible; may exacerbate myasthenia gravis; C. difficile colitis risk with antibiotics.
