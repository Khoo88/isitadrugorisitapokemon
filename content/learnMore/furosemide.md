## What is Furosemide?

Furosemide is a loop diuretic that inhibits the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, preventing sodium and chloride reabsorption and producing potent diuresis. It increases excretion of sodium, potassium, chloride, magnesium, and water.

Rapid oral and intravenous onset makes it essential for acute pulmonary edema and severe fluid overload. High ceiling effect allows increased dosing when response plateaus, though ototoxicity risk rises with rapid high-dose IV administration.

Renal elimination; efficacy may decrease in advanced renal failure (high-dose or combination strategies required). It activates renin-angiotensin system, relevant when co-prescribing ACE inhibitors.

## Primary Uses

- Acute decompensated heart failure and pulmonary edema
- Edema associated with chronic heart failure, cirrhosis, or nephrotic syndrome
- Acute kidney injury with hypervolemia (careful hemodynamic management)
- Hypertension (less common than thiazides for chronic management)
- Hypercalcemia (promotes calciuresis, adjunct)

## Common Side Effects

- Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, metabolic alkalosis
- Hypotension, dehydration, acute kidney injury if over-diuresed
- Ototoxicity (tinnitus, hearing loss—especially rapid IV high doses)
- Hyperuricemia (gout flares)
- Hyperglycemia
- **Warnings:** Monitor electrolytes and renal function; allergy to sulfa drugs (cross-reactivity possible but often tolerated); photosensitivity; contraindicated in anuria; monitor lithium levels if co-administered.
