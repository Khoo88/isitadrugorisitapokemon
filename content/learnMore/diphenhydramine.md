## What is Diphenhydramine?

Diphenhydramine is a first-generation ethanolamine antihistamine (H1 receptor antagonist) that crosses the blood-brain barrier, causing marked sedative and anticholinergic effects. It competes with histamine at H1 receptors, reducing allergic symptoms such as urticaria, rhinorrhea, and pruritus.

It also inhibits muscarinic acetylcholine receptors, producing drying of secretions and sedation used in insomnia products (though not preferred for chronic sleep disorders). IV use is established for acute allergic reactions and as adjunct antiemetic.

Short duration of action requires frequent dosing for allergy; many guidelines discourage use in older adults due to anticholinergic burden (Beers Criteria).

## Primary Uses

- Allergic rhinitis, urticaria, angioedema adjunct (mild cases)
- Anaphylaxis adjunct therapy (after epinephrine, not as monotherapy)
- Motion sickness prophylaxis
- Insomnia (short-term, OTC—non-preferred vs. behavioral therapy)
- Parkinson disease extrapyramidal symptoms (acute dystonic reactions, IV/IM)
- Cough/cold combination products (symptomatic relief)

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, drowsiness
- Dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention (anticholinergic)
- Confusion, delirium (elderly)
- Dizziness
- Paradoxical excitation in children
- **Warnings:** Avoid driving; caution in glaucoma, BPH, asthma; high overdose risk with anticholinergic toxicity; not recommended as hypnotic in elderly; many drug-drug interactions via sedation.
