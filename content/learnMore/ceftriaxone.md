## What is Ceftriaxone?

Ceftriaxone is a third-generation cephalosporin antibiotic that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins. It has a broad spectrum against many gram-negative organisms and good gram-positive activity (excluding methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus and Enterococcus). Long half-life allows once- or twice-daily parenteral dosing.

High protein binding and biliary excretion of a significant portion distinguish it from other cephalosporins; caution is advised with concurrent calcium-containing IV solutions in neonates (precipitation risk). It crosses the blood-brain barrier in inflamed meninges, supporting use in bacterial meningitis.

Renal elimination accounts for remainder; dose adjustment in severe renal failure per institutional guidance.

## Primary Uses

- Community-acquired pneumonia (inpatient)
- Complicated urinary tract infections and pyelonephritis
- Gonorrhea (single intramuscular dose per CDC guidelines, with test-of-cure considerations)
- Bacterial meningitis (with vancomycin or ampicillin depending on age and pathogens)
- Skin and soft tissue infections (cellulitis, moderate severity)
- Sepsis and intra-abdominal infections (combination regimens)
- Lyme disease (neurologic or disseminated, per guidelines)
- Surgical prophylaxis (selected procedures)

## Common Side Effects

- Pain at injection site (intramuscular)
- Diarrhea, nausea
- Rash, drug fever
- Elevated liver enzymes, biliary sludge/pseudolithiasis (usually reversible)
- Hemolytic anemia (rare)
- Clostridioides difficile colitis
- **Warnings:** Cross-allergy with penicillin in ~1–2% true IgE cases—assess allergy history; avoid calcium co-infusion in neonates; prolonged use may cause gallbladder complications; superinfection risk.
