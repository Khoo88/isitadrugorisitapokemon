## What is Naltrexone?

Naltrexone is a competitive opioid antagonist at mu and kappa receptors used orally daily or as monthly intramuscular extended-release injection for alcohol and opioid use disorders. For alcohol, it reduces craving and heavy drinking days through endogenous opioid pathway modulation rather than causing disulfiram-like reactions.

For opioid use disorder, patients must be opioid-free for 7–10 days before initiation to avoid precipitated withdrawal (longer for methadone or buprenorphine). It blocks euphoric effects if opioids are used, supporting abstinence.

Hepatotoxicity at high doses was historically concerning; standard doses have low hepatotoxicity risk but liver function is monitored. It is not an opioid and is not a controlled substance.

## Primary Uses

- Alcohol use disorder (reduction of heavy drinking, relapse prevention)
- Opioid use disorder maintenance (after detoxification, motivated patients)
- Extended-release injection for adherence in opioid and alcohol use disorders
- Behavioral addiction research (off-label, limited)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps
- Headache, dizziness, fatigue
- Insomnia, anxiety
- Hepatotoxicity (elevated transaminases—monitor)
- Injection-site reactions (extended-release)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated on current opioids or in acute withdrawal; carry warning card for emergency analgesia (opioids may require higher doses or alternative analgesia); precipitated withdrawal if opioids present; avoid in acute hepatitis or liver failure.
