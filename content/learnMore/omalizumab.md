## What is Omalizumab?

Omalizumab is a recombinant humanized monoclonal anti-immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibody that binds free IgE in circulation, preventing IgE attachment to high-affinity FcεRI receptors on mast cells and basophils. Reduced receptor-bound IgE limits allergen-triggered degranulation and downstream inflammatory cascades.

Subcutaneous dosing every 2–4 weeks is weight- and total IgE–based (within labeled ranges). It does not treat acute bronchospasm. Response in asthma may take months; pretreatment IgE level guides eligibility.

Used in moderate to severe allergic asthma uncontrolled on inhaled corticosteroids and in chronic spontaneous urticaria refractory to antihistamines.

## Primary Uses

- Moderate to severe persistent allergic asthma (IgE-mediated, age ≥6 per labeling)
- Chronic spontaneous urticaria inadequately controlled by H1 antihistamines
- Nasal polyps with chronic rhinosinusitis (add-on, approved indication)
- Food allergy (emerging protocols—specialist settings, not acute reaction treatment)

Anaphylaxis risk exists with injection; administration in monitored settings initially per guidelines.

## Common Side Effects

- Injection-site reactions
- Headache, upper respiratory infections
- Arthralgia, fatigue
- Anaphylaxis (rare, can be delayed)
- Malignancy signals debated in observational data (no definitive causal proof)
- **Warnings:** Observe patient after injection per protocol; not for acute asthma attacks; do not discontinue inhaled corticosteroids abruptly; cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events monitored in trials.
