## What is Piperacillin?

Piperacillin is an extended-spectrum ureidopenicillin beta-lactam antibiotic that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. It is most commonly co-formulated with tazobactam, a beta-lactamase inhibitor that extends activity against beta-lactamase–producing gram-negative organisms and some anaerobes.

Piperacillin-tazobactam provides broad coverage for intra-abdominal, skin, pneumonia, and sepsis sources including Pseudomonas aeruginosa in many institutions, though resistance patterns vary. Parenteral administration only; renal elimination requires dose adjustment.

Penicillin allergy assessment is required; cross-reactivity with cephalosporins is low for IgE-mediated allergy in many patients but institutional protocols vary.

## Primary Uses

- Complicated intra-abdominal infections
- Hospital-acquired and ventilator-associated pneumonia (combination regimens)
- Complicated urinary tract infections and pyelonephritis
- Skin and soft tissue infections (severe)
- Febrile neutropenia (empiric, per protocol)
- Sepsis with suspected polymicrobial or resistant gram-negative sources

Often combined with vancomycin or other agents for MRSA coverage when indicated.

## Common Side Effects

- Diarrhea, nausea
- Rash, drug fever
- Elevated liver enzymes
- Clostridioides difficile colitis
- Bleeding tendency (piperacillin antiplatelet effect—rare, high doses)
- **Warnings:** Adjust in renal impairment; sodium load in high doses; seizure risk in renal failure; major allergy assessment for beta-lactams.
