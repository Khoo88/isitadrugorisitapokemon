## What is Isoniazid?

Isoniazid (INH) is a bactericidal antimycobacterial agent that inhibits mycolic acid synthesis in Mycobacterium tuberculosis cell walls, disrupting envelope integrity. It is a cornerstone of all standard tuberculosis regimens and latent TB treatment.

Hepatotoxicity—from asymptomatic transaminase elevation to fulminant hepatitis—is the major adverse effect; monthly clinical monitoring and patient education on symptoms are standard. Pyridoxine (vitamin B6) supplementation prevents peripheral neuropathy, especially in malnutrition, pregnancy, diabetes, and HIV.

It is metabolized by N-acetyltransferase 2 (slow vs. fast acetylators affect toxicity and dosing in some protocols). Drug interactions include increased phenytoin and disulfiram effects.

## Primary Uses

- Active pulmonary and extrapulmonary tuberculosis (with rifampin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol in initial phase)
- Latent tuberculosis infection treatment
- Tuberculosis prophylaxis in close contacts (per public health)

Always used as part of combination therapy for active disease to prevent resistance.

## Common Side Effects

- Hepatotoxicity (elevated ALT/AST, jaundice—discontinue if symptomatic or marked elevation)
- Peripheral neuropathy (prevent with pyridoxine)
- Rash, drug fever
- Gastrointestinal upset
- Seizures (overdose or predisposed patients)
- Lupus-like syndrome (anti-histone antibodies)
- **Warnings:** Avoid alcohol; monitor liver function; educate on hepatitis symptoms; adjust in severe liver disease; interactions with rifampin (both hepatotoxic—enhanced monitoring).
