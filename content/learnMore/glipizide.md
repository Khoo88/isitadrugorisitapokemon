## What is Glipizide?

Glipizide is a second-generation sulfonylurea that stimulates insulin secretion from pancreatic beta cells by binding SUR1 subunits of ATP-sensitive potassium channels, causing membrane depolarization, calcium influx, and insulin release. Efficacy requires residual beta-cell function.

It lowers fasting and postprandial glucose but carries hypoglycemia risk, especially in elderly patients, renal impairment, or missed meals. Weight gain is common compared with metformin or GLP-1 agonists.

Metabolized by hepatic cytochrome enzymes to inactive metabolites; renal excretion of metabolites contributes to elimination. Shorter duration than glyburide makes it a commonly chosen sulfonylurea.

## Primary Uses

- Type 2 diabetes mellitus when lifestyle and metformin are insufficient (add-on or alternative per guidelines)
- Short-term glucose control in selected patients unable to use other agents

Sulfonylureas are generally second- or third-line after metformin and newer agents with cardiovascular benefit in many guidelines.

## Common Side Effects

- Hypoglycemia (most clinically significant)
- Weight gain
- Nausea, gastrointestinal upset
- Rash (sulfonamide cross-sensitivity rare)
- Hyponatremia (SIADH—uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Counsel on hypoglycemia recognition; avoid in significant renal or hepatic failure when safer alternatives exist; disulfiram-like reaction not typical with glipizide (unlike some older agents); cardiovascular safety debated but widely used.
