## Who is Alomomola?

Alomomola is a Water-type Pokémon from Generation V, resembling a giant pink ocean sunfish (mola mola) with caring, maternal overtones. Despite its fishlike appearance, it is not part of the same evolutionary family as Luvdisc or other heart-themed species—Alomomola stands alone as a gentle leviathan of warm seas.

In the Pokémon universe, Alomomola is widely regarded as a natural healer of the deep. Coastal communities in Unova and beyond feature it in stories about companions who guide injured Pokémon back to shore. Its serene expression and slow, drifting movement define its place as a peaceful guardian rather than a predator.

## Biological Quirks & Lore

Pokédex lore highlights specialized fins that sense distress in nearby creatures, prompting Alomomola to envelop wounded partners and share regenerative body fluids. Some entries describe it swimming alongside schools for days, radiating calm that discourages aggression from would-be hunters.

Biologically, its thick blubber and redundant organs allow survival in open ocean where food is scarce. When threatened, it can dive to remarkable depths and “play dead,” drifting with currents until danger passes—a behavior mirroring real mola mola survival strategies.

## Battle Performance

Alomomola excels as a defensive pivot with enormous HP, Regenerator, and recovery via Wish and Protect. Moves like Scald, Toxic, and Knock Off let it stall offensive teams while keeping allies healthy through double-battle support or passing wishes to teammates.

It lacks offensive pressure but can wear down foes with status and repeated healing. In formats where slow, bulky Water-types are viable, Alomomola remains a niche yet frustrating wall—especially when paired with entry hazard removal and special walls.
