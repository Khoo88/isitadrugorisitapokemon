## What is Hydrochlorothiazide?

Hydrochlorothiazide (HCTZ) is a thiazide diuretic that inhibits sodium-chloride cotransport in the distal convoluted tubule, increasing urinary sodium and water excretion. Chronic use reduces blood pressure through initial volume loss and subsequent vasodilatory effects.

It is among the most prescribed antihypertensives worldwide, often combined with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or calcium channel blockers in single-pill combinations. Electrolyte disturbances—hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hypomagnesemia—are class effects requiring periodic monitoring.

Thiazides increase uric acid and may unmask gout; they can raise blood glucose. Sulfa allergy cross-reactivity is possible but often tolerated.

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension (first-line thiazide or thiazide-like therapy in many guidelines)
- Edema associated with heart failure, renal disease, or corticosteroid therapy
- Nephrolithiasis prevention in hypercalciuria (reduces urinary calcium)
- Diabetes insipidus (paradoxical antidiuresis at low doses)
- Osteoporosis risk reduction (modest increase in bone density with long-term use)

## Common Side Effects

- Hypokalemia, hyponatremia
- Hyperuricemia (gout)
- Hyperglycemia
- Dizziness, orthostatic hypotension
- Photosensitivity rash
- Impaired renal function with volume depletion
- **Warnings:** Monitor electrolytes and renal function; use cautiously in gout and severe renal impairment (loop diuretics preferred when GFR very low); lithium levels may increase.
