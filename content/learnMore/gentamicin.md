## What is Gentamicin?

Gentamicin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by irreversibly binding the 30S ribosomal subunit, causing misreading of mRNA. It is bactericidal against aerobic gram-negative bacilli, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and has synergy with beta-lactams against some gram-positive organisms.

Concentration-dependent killing and post-antibiotic effect support once-daily dosing in many protocols. Therapeutic drug monitoring (peak/trough or area-under-curve strategies) guides dosing to maximize efficacy and minimize nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity.

Poor oral absorption limits use to parenteral, topical, and inhaled (selected) routes. It does not penetrate cerebrospinal fluid well unless meninges are inflamed.

## Primary Uses

- Serious gram-negative infections (sepsis, pneumonia, urinary tract, intra-abdominal—combination regimens)
- Endocarditis (synergy with beta-lactams for enterococci and streptococci per protocols)
- Bone and joint infections (combination therapy)
- Topical therapy for infected burns and wound infections
- Surgical prophylaxis (selected regimens, institutional)

## Common Side Effects

- Nephrotoxicity (acute tubular necrosis, often reversible)
- Ototoxicity (vestibular and cochlear—may be irreversible)
- Neuromuscular blockade (caution with anesthesia)
- **Warnings:** Avoid concurrent nephrotoxins when possible; monitor levels and renal function; single daily dosing preferred; not for uncomplicated cystitis monotherapy; pregnancy category risk for fetal ototoxicity.
