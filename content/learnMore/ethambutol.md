## What is Ethambutol?

Ethambutol is a bacteriostatic antimycobacterial agent that inhibits arabinosyl transferase enzymes involved in mycobacterial cell wall arabinogalactan synthesis, disrupting cell wall assembly. It is active against Mycobacterium tuberculosis and nontuberculous mycobacteria as part of combination therapy to prevent resistance.

The hallmark toxicity is dose-dependent optic neuropathy affecting color vision (red-green discrimination) and visual acuity, often reversible if detected early. Monthly visual symptom screening and formal ophthalmologic assessment protocols are used in long-term therapy.

Renal elimination requires dose adjustment in kidney disease. It is a cornerstone of standard four-drug initial tuberculosis regimens alongside isoniazid, rifampin, and pyrazinamide.

## Primary Uses

- Active pulmonary and extrapulmonary tuberculosis (combination therapy)
- Mycobacterium avium complex infection (multi-drug regimens)
- Latent tuberculosis is not treated with ethambutol alone

Always prescribed as part of directly observed or multidrug protocols per public health guidelines.

## Common Side Effects

- Optic neuritis (decreased visual acuity, color vision defects—dose-related)
- Peripheral neuropathy (uncommon)
- Rash, pruritus
- Gastrointestinal upset
- Hyperuricemia (usually asymptomatic)
- **Warnings:** Baseline and periodic vision assessment; contraindicated or use extreme caution if unable to report visual changes; adjust dose in renal impairment; discontinue immediately if optic toxicity confirmed.
