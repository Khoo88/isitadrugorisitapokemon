## What is Fingolimod?

Fingolimod is a sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulator that phosphorylates and functionally antagonizes S1P receptors on lymphocytes, preventing egress from lymph nodes and reducing circulation of autoreactive lymphocytes into the central nervous system. It is used in relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis.

First-dose bradycardia occurs because S1P receptors on cardiac atrial tissue are affected; initial dosing requires monitoring. Metabolism yields active metabolites; ophthalmologic macular edema and reduced pulmonary function are class concerns.

Fingolimod was the first oral disease-modifying therapy widely adopted for MS before newer S1P modulators (siponimod, ozanimod) with more selective receptor profiles.

## Primary Uses

- Relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (reduction of relapses and MRI lesion activity)
- Clinically isolated syndrome (selected patients, per neurology criteria)

Not for primary progressive MS without relapses. Vaccination status should be reviewed before initiation—live vaccines avoided during therapy.

## Common Side Effects

- Headache
- Elevated liver transaminases
- Diarrhea, nausea
- First-dose bradycardia and atrioventricular block (monitoring required)
- Macular edema (ophthalmologic screening recommended)
- Hypertension
- Respiratory effects (reduced forced expiratory volume)
- Infections (herpes zoster, other)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in recent myocardial infarction, certain arrhythmias, and active macular edema; do not discontinue abruptly without specialist guidance; progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy risk with immunosuppression (rare); pregnancy contraindicated.
