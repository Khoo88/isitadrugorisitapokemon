## What is Kevzara?

Kevzara (sarilumab) is a fully human monoclonal antibody that binds the interleukin-6 (IL-6) receptor, blocking IL-6-mediated signaling involved in acute-phase reactants, synovial inflammation, and systemic symptoms in rheumatoid arthritis. IL-6 inhibition reduces C-reactive protein, joint damage progression, and constitutional symptoms.

Subcutaneous administration every two weeks follows loading dose. Like other biologic immunosuppressants, it increases infection risk, including reactivation of tuberculosis and opportunistic infections; screening before initiation is required.

Elevated liver enzymes and neutropenia can occur; lipid increases have been observed. It competes in the same therapeutic space as tocilizumab (another IL-6 pathway agent with different target).

## Primary Uses

- Moderate to severe rheumatoid arthritis inadequately responding to one or more disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs), including methotrexate
- Giant cell arteritis (approved indication in some regions)

Used when conventional DMARDs and sometimes TNF inhibitors are insufficient; polyarticular course and serologic markers guide specialist prescribing.

## Common Side Effects

- Injection-site reactions
- Upper respiratory tract infections
- Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
- Elevated liver transaminases
- Lipid elevations
- Serious infections (pneumonia, opportunistic)
- **Warnings:** TB and hepatitis B screening; avoid live vaccines; do not start during active infection; monitor CBC and liver function; bowel perforation reported (rare, caution in diverticulitis history).
