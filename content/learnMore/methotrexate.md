## What is Methotrexate?

Methotrexate (MTX) is an antimetabolite that inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, impairing DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing cells. At low weekly doses used in rheumatology and dermatology, anti-inflammatory effects predominate through adenosine pathway modulation and effects on cytokine-producing cells.

High-dose regimens with leucovorin rescue treat malignancies and ectopic pregnancy. Low-dose weekly oral or subcutaneous therapy is anchor treatment for rheumatoid arthritis and psoriasis; folic acid supplementation reduces mucosal and hematologic toxicity.

Hepatotoxicity, myelosuppression, and pulmonary toxicity require monitoring. It is teratogenic (pregnancy category X); contraception is mandatory.

## Primary Uses

- Rheumatoid arthritis (first-line DMARD)
- Psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis
- Inflammatory bowel disease (moderate to severe, specialist use)
- Juvenile idiopathic arthritis
- Cancer chemotherapy (high-dose protocols)
- Ectopic pregnancy (single-dose or multidose protocols)
- Uveitis (with ophthalmology)

Alcohol and hepatotoxic drugs increase liver risk; NSAIDs may increase MTX toxicity at high NSAID doses.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, stomatitis, oral ulcers
- Elevated liver enzymes (fibrosis with chronic use—monitor)
- Myelosuppression (neutropenia, anemia)
- Fatigue, headache
- Pulmonary toxicity (hypersensitivity pneumonitis, fibrosis—rare)
- **Warnings:** Weekly dosing only (daily dosing errors are fatal); folic acid supplementation; avoid pregnancy; monitor CBC and LFTs; major interaction with trimethoprim (pancytopenia); live vaccines avoided.
