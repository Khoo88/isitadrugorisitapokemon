## What is Acetaminophen?

Acetaminophen (paracetamol) is an analgesic and antipyretic agent widely used for mild to moderate pain and fever. Unlike nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), it has minimal anti-inflammatory activity at standard doses. Its exact mechanism is not fully defined but is thought to involve central inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX) variants, particularly COX-2, and modulation of serotonergic and cannabinoid pathways in the central nervous system.

The drug is extensively metabolized in the liver, primarily via glucuronidation and sulfation. A minor oxidative pathway via CYP2E1 produces the toxic metabolite N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine (NAPQI), which is normally detoxified by glutathione. In overdose, glutathione stores are depleted, leading to hepatocellular injury. Acetaminophen is available over the counter and in combination products; total daily dose limits are critical to prevent hepatotoxicity.

Therapeutically, it is favored when NSAIDs are contraindicated—such as in patients with gastric ulcer disease, renal impairment, or anticoagulant use—though it does not provide the anti-inflammatory or antiplatelet effects of NSAIDs.

## Primary Uses

- Relief of mild to moderate pain (headache, dental pain, musculoskeletal pain, postoperative pain as part of multimodal analgesia)
- Reduction of fever in adults and children
- Osteoarthritis pain management when anti-inflammatory effect is not required
- Component of combination regimens for moderate pain (e.g., with opioids in acute settings, per prescribing guidelines)

It is a first-line antipyretic in many pediatric and adult guidelines. Providers emphasize maximum daily limits (typically 3,000–4,000 mg/day in healthy adults, lower in liver disease or chronic alcohol use) and caution against duplicate dosing from combination cold and pain products.

## Common Side Effects

- Generally well tolerated at therapeutic doses
- Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain (more common in overdose)
- Hepatotoxicity with acute overdose or chronic excessive use (elevated transaminases, jaundice, acute liver failure)
- Rare hypersensitivity reactions (rash, angioedema)
- Chronic high-dose use may be associated with renal effects in some populations
- **Warnings:** Avoid exceeding recommended daily limits; caution in liver disease, chronic alcohol use, malnutrition, and when combined with other acetaminophen-containing products. N-acetylcysteine is the antidote for significant overdose.
