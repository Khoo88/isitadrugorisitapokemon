## What is Promethazine?

Promethazine is a phenothiazine derivative with strong H1-antihistamine, moderate anticholinergic, and dopamine D2 antagonist properties. It crosses the blood-brain barrier, producing sedation, antiemetic effects, and motion sickness prophylaxis. Anti-nausea action involves central H1 and muscarinic blockade and dopamine antagonism in the chemoreceptor trigger zone.

IV administration carries black box warnings for severe tissue injury with extravasation and respiratory depression in children; IV use is restricted or contraindicated in young children per FDA labeling. Oral and rectal routes remain common for nausea and sedation.

It potentiates CNS depressants and has significant anticholinergic burden in elderly patients.

## Primary Uses

- Nausea and vomiting (postoperative, gastroenteritis adjunct, chemotherapy adjunct)
- Motion sickness prophylaxis
- Sedation before procedures (adjunct)
- Allergic rhinitis and urticaria (second-line to nonsedating antihistamines)
- Nighttime cough/cold combination products (symptomatic)

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, drowsiness
- Dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention
- Confusion, delirium (elderly)
- Extrapyramidal reactions (uncommon)
- Respiratory depression (especially with other CNS depressants)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated in children under 2 years; IV extravasation can cause gangrene; photosensitivity; avoid with MAO inhibitors; Beers Criteria—avoid in elderly for insomnia.
