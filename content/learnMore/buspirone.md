## What is Buspirone?

Buspirone is an anxiolytic agent that acts as a partial agonist at serotonin 5-HT1A receptors and may modulate dopamine receptors. Unlike benzodiazepines, it does not bind GABAA receptors and is not a controlled substance. It lacks sedative, muscle relaxant, and anticonvulsant properties typical of benzodiazepines and does not cause physiologic dependence at usual doses.

Onset of anxiolytic effect is delayed (2–4 weeks), similar to antidepressants, limiting use for acute panic. Metabolism via CYP3A4 produces active metabolites; grapefruit juice increases levels.

It is often chosen when benzodiazepine avoidance is desired, though efficacy in generalized anxiety disorder is modest compared with SSRIs in some trials.

## Primary Uses

- Generalized anxiety disorder (chronic management)
- Short-term augmentation or bridge therapy in anxiety when avoiding benzodiazepines
- Adjunctive treatment of depression-related anxiety (off-label, variable evidence)

Not effective for panic disorder monotherapy in many patients and not a treatment for benzodiazepine withdrawal seizures.

## Common Side Effects

- Dizziness, lightheadedness
- Nausea, headache
- Nervousness, restlessness (early therapy)
- Sedation (mild, less than benzodiazepines)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated with MAO inhibitors (serotonin syndrome risk); may require dose reduction with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors; full effect requires consistent daily dosing for weeks.
