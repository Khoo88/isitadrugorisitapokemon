## What is Lenvima?

Lenvima (lenvatinib) is an oral multikinase inhibitor targeting vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFR1–3), fibroblast growth factor receptors, platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha, KIT, and RET. By blocking angiogenic and oncogenic signaling pathways, it slows tumor growth in differentiated thyroid cancer, renal cell carcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma.

It is taken once daily with or without food. Hypertension is a class effect of VEGF inhibition, requiring proactive blood pressure management. Proteinuria, diarrhea, and fatigue are common dose-limiting toxicities.

Metabolism involves CYP3A4; strong inducers and inhibitors affect exposure. Unlike some earlier tyrosine kinase inhibitors, it has a distinct toxicity profile manageable with dose holds and reductions.

## Primary Uses

- Differentiated thyroid cancer refractory to radioactive iodine
- Advanced renal cell carcinoma (combination with everolimus)
- Unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (first-line, often with pembrolizumab per current protocols)
- Advanced endometrial carcinoma (combination with pembrolizumab, selected populations)

Prescribed by oncology specialists with monitoring protocols for blood pressure, liver function, and thyroid function.

## Common Side Effects

- Hypertension
- Diarrhea, nausea, decreased appetite, weight loss
- Fatigue, asthenia
- Palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia (hand-foot syndrome)
- Proteinuria, renal impairment
- Headache, dysphonia
- Thromboembolic events, hemorrhage, gastrointestinal perforation (serious, uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Monitor BP weekly initially; thyroid and renal monitoring; wound healing delay—hold before surgery; hepatotoxicity; QT prolongation at high exposure.
