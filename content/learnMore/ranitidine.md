## What is Ranitidine?

Ranitidine is a histamine H2-receptor antagonist that competitively blocks H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells, reducing basal and stimulated gastric acid secretion. It is less potent and shorter-acting than proton pump inhibitors but works faster for symptom relief than PPIs in some patients.

It was widely used for peptic ulcer disease, GERD, and stress ulcer prophylaxis until voluntary withdrawals and FDA requests removed many products from the U.S. market due to N-nitrosodimethylamine (NDMA) contamination concerns in manufacturing. Reformulated or alternate H2 blockers (famotidine) largely replaced clinical use.

Where available, metabolism is hepatic with renal elimination of metabolites; drug interactions are fewer than with cimetidine.

## Primary Uses

- Peptic ulcer disease (historical; famotidine or PPIs now preferred where ranitidine unavailable)
- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (symptomatic relief)
- Erosive esophagitis (milder cases, second-line to PPIs)
- Stress ulcer prophylaxis in critical illness (historical; PPIs common now)

Clinical availability varies by country and reformulation status.

## Common Side Effects

- Headache, dizziness
- Constipation or diarrhea
- Rare: hepatotoxicity, blood dyscrasias
- Confusion in elderly (CNS penetration)
- **Warnings:** Verify current product availability and NDMA testing if prescribed; adjust dose in renal impairment; less effective than PPIs for erosive GERD; drug interactions less than cimetidine but still present.
