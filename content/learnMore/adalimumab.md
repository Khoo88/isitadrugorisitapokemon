## What is Adalimumab?

Adalimumab is a fully human monoclonal immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1) antibody that binds specifically to tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α), neutralizing its biologic activity. TNF-α is a proinflammatory cytokine central to the pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, inflammatory bowel disease, and plaque psoriasis.

By blocking TNF-α interaction with cell surface receptors, adalimumab reduces downstream signaling involving nuclear factor-κB, decreasing production of additional cytokines, matrix metalloproteinases, and adhesion molecules. It is administered subcutaneously and has a half-life of approximately two weeks, supporting every-other-week or weekly dosing depending on indication.

As a biologic immunomodulator, it increases susceptibility to serious infections, including reactivation of latent tuberculosis and invasive fungal infections. Screening for TB and hepatitis B is standard before initiation.

## Primary Uses

- Rheumatoid arthritis (moderate to severe, often with methotrexate)
- Psoriatic arthritis
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis (moderate to severe)
- Plaque psoriasis
- Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (polyarticular)
- Hidradenitis suppurativa and uveitis (selected indications)

Prescribing is typically limited to specialists or clinicians experienced with biologics, with step therapy through conventional disease-modifying agents in many health systems.

## Common Side Effects

- Injection-site reactions (erythema, pain, pruritus)
- Upper respiratory tract infections
- Headache, rash
- Increased risk of serious infections (bacterial, viral, opportunistic)
- Latent tuberculosis reactivation
- Heart failure exacerbation in susceptible patients
- Demyelinating disease (rare)
- Malignancy concerns with long-term immunosuppression (lymphoma and others, debated in context of underlying disease)
- **Warnings:** Test for latent TB and hepatitis B; avoid live vaccines; discontinue if serious infection or neurologic symptoms develop; caution in heart failure class III/IV.
