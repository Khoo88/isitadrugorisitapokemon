## What is Famotidine?

Famotidine is a histamine H2-receptor antagonist that competitively blocks histamine at H2 receptors on gastric parietal cells, reducing basal and stimulated gastric acid secretion. It lowers intragastric acidity, promoting healing of peptic ulcers and relieving gastroesophageal reflux symptoms, though proton pump inhibitors are more potent acid suppressants for erosive disease.

Hepatic metabolism is minimal; renal excretion predominates, requiring dose reduction in renal impairment. Unlike cimetidine, famotidine has few cytochrome P450 interactions and minimal antiandrogenic effects.

Onset is relatively rapid compared with PPIs, making it useful for intermittent symptomatic relief.

## Primary Uses

- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (mild to moderate symptoms, intermittent use)
- Peptic ulcer disease (duodenal and gastric ulcer healing and maintenance)
- Erosive esophagitis (milder cases; PPIs preferred for severe erosive disease)
- Stress ulcer prophylaxis in hospitalized patients (selected protocols, though PPIs often preferred)
- Urticaria adjunct (off-label, less potent than H1 blockers)

Available OTC and prescription strengths.

## Common Side Effects

- Headache
- Dizziness, constipation, diarrhea
- Fatigue
- Rare: reversible confusional states (especially in elderly or renal failure)
- **Warnings:** Dose adjust in creatinine clearance <50 mL/min; long-term acid suppression may affect vitamin B12 and magnesium (class observation stronger with PPIs); may mask symptoms of gastric malignancy—evaluate alarm features; drug interaction profile mild compared with cimetidine.
