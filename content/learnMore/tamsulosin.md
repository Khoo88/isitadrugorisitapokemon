## What is Tamsulosin?

Tamsulosin is an alpha-1 adrenergic receptor antagonist with relative selectivity for alpha-1A receptors in the prostate and bladder neck, relaxing smooth muscle and improving urine flow in benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). Unlike nonselective alpha-blockers (terazosin, doxazosin), it has less blood pressure lowering at usual doses.

Rapid onset of urinary symptom relief contrasts with 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride), which shrink the prostate over months. Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome is associated with alpha-blockers—ophthalmology should be informed before cataract surgery.

Hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4 and CYP2D6; strong inhibitors increase exposure.

## Primary Uses

- Lower urinary tract symptoms due to benign prostatic hyperplasia
- Ureteral stone expulsion adjunct (off-label, facilitates passage in distal stones per some trials)
- Chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome (adjunct, limited evidence)

## Common Side Effects

- Dizziness, orthostatic hypotension (less than nonselective agents)
- Retrograde ejaculation (abnormal ejaculation)
- Rhinitis, headache
- Fatigue
- Intraoperative floppy iris syndrome during cataract surgery
- **Warnings:** First-dose hypotension less common than prazosin but still caution; not for use as antihypertensive; inform surgeon before eye surgery; priapism (rare).
