## What is Valacyclovir?

Valacyclovir is an L-valine ester prodrug of acyclovir with substantially improved oral bioavailability (approximately 55% vs. 15% for acyclovir). After absorption, hepatic and intestinal conversion releases acyclovir, which is phosphorylated by viral thymidine kinase in HSV- and VZV-infected cells to inhibit viral DNA polymerase.

Less frequent dosing supports adherence for genital herpes suppression and shingles treatment. Renal elimination of acyclovir requires dose adjustment in renal impairment and adequate hydration to prevent crystalline nephropathy at high doses.

Resistance mechanisms mirror acyclovir (deficient thymidine kinase, altered polymerase).

## Primary Uses

- Genital herpes (initial episode, recurrent episodes, chronic suppression)
- Herpes labialis (cold sores)
- Herpes zoster (shingles)
- Varicella (chickenpox, age/weight-based dosing)
- Cytomegalovirus prophylaxis in transplantation (off-label high-dose protocols)

## Common Side Effects

- Headache, nausea
- Abdominal pain
- Dizziness
- Renal impairment with dehydration or high doses
- Neurotoxicity in renal failure (confusion, tremor—rare)
- **Warnings:** Adjust dose by creatinine clearance; maintain hydration; caution in immunocompromised patients with severe disease requiring IV therapy; pregnancy—generally used when benefit outweighs risk.
