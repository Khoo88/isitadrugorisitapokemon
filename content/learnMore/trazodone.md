## What is Trazodone?

Trazodone is a serotonin antagonist and reuptake inhibitor (SARI) that blocks 5-HT2A receptors and inhibits serotonin reuptake at higher doses, with additional H1 and alpha-1 antagonism causing sedation. At low doses it is widely used for insomnia; at higher doses it treats major depression.

Minimal anticholinergic activity compared with tricyclics. Orthostatic hypotension and priapism (rare but urologic emergency) are notable adverse effects.

Metabolized to active m-chlorophenylpiperazine (mCPP); CYP3A4 interactions are clinically relevant.

## Primary Uses

- Major depressive disorder (less common today than SSRIs)
- Insomnia (low-dose, most common current use)
- Anxiety (adjunct, off-label)
- Alcohol withdrawal sleep disturbance (adjunct, institutional protocols)

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, morning grogginess
- Orthostatic hypotension, dizziness
- Dry mouth, blurred vision (mild)
- Priapism (rare—seek emergency care)
- Headache
- **Warnings:** Priapism counseling; additive sedation with alcohol and CNS depressants; QT prolongation at high doses or with interacting drugs; suicidality monitoring when used for depression; taper if used chronically at antidepressant doses.
