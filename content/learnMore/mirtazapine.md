## What is Mirtazapine?

Mirtazapine is an atypical antidepressant that antagonizes central alpha-2 autoreceptors and heteroreceptors, increasing norepinephrine and serotonin release, and blocks 5-HT2 and 5-HT3 receptors and H1 histamine receptors. Net effect enhances noradrenergic and specific serotonergic transmission (NaSSA class).

Sedation and appetite stimulation from H1 blockade make it useful in depressed patients with insomnia, weight loss, or nausea, especially in elderly patients at lower doses. It has minimal sexual side effects compared with SSRIs.

Metabolism via CYP1A2, 2D6, and 3A4; drug interactions are fewer than some antidepressants but still relevant.

## Primary Uses

- Major depressive disorder
- Appetite stimulation and weight gain in depression or cachexia (adjunct)
- Insomnia associated with depression (sedating dose at night)
- Anxiety symptoms with depression (adjunct evidence)

## Common Side Effects

- Somnolence, increased appetite, weight gain
- Dry mouth
- Dizziness
- Constipation
- Elevated cholesterol and triglycerides (long-term)
- Agranulocytosis (rare)
- **Warnings:** Monitor for worsening depression/suicidality (class warning); serotonin syndrome with MAOIs and serotonergic drugs; avoid abrupt discontinuation; caution in bipolar disorder (may trigger mania).
