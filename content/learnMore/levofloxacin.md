## What is Levofloxacin?

Levofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic, the L-isomer of ofloxacin, that inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, preventing DNA replication. It is bactericidal with broad gram-negative activity, including atypical pathogens (Legionella, Mycoplasma, Chlamydia), and good gram-positive coverage including Streptococcus pneumoniae in many communities.

Excellent oral bioavailability achieves serum levels comparable to intravenous administration. Fluoroquinolones carry FDA warnings regarding tendinitis, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects, and aortic aneurysm/dissection risk with systemic use; prescribing should be reserved for indications where benefits outweigh risks.

QT prolongation and glucose dysregulation (hypo- and hyperglycemia) are class concerns.

## Primary Uses

- Community-acquired pneumonia (outpatient and inpatient when indicated)
- Acute bacterial sinusitis and exacerbations of chronic bronchitis (when alternatives inappropriate per guidelines)
- Complicated urinary tract infections and pyelonephritis
- Prostatitis
- Skin and soft tissue infections (selected)
- Anthrax post-exposure (alternative)
- Plague (Yersinia pestis, public health)

Many guidelines restrict fluoroquinolone use when other effective antibiotics exist.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, diarrhea
- Headache, dizziness
- Tendinitis, tendon rupture (especially Achilles, corticosteroid co-use)
- Peripheral neuropathy (may be irreversible)
- QT prolongation
- CNS effects (confusion, seizures)
- Photosensitivity
- **Warnings:** FDA boxed warnings for serious adverse effects; avoid in myasthenia gravis; reserve for indicated infections; major drug interactions (antacids, warfarin, QT drugs).
