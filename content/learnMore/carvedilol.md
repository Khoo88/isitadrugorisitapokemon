## What is Carvedilol?

Carvedilol is a non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker with alpha-1 adrenergic blocking activity, classifying it as a third-generation beta-blocker. Beta-blockade reduces heart rate, contractility, and renin release; alpha-1 blockade causes vasodilation, offsetting unopposed alpha vasoconstriction and contributing to afterload reduction.

It is racemic; both S and R enantiomers contribute to beta and alpha effects. Carvedilol also exhibits antioxidant properties in experimental models. It is extensively metabolized by the liver; food increases bioavailability of immediate-release formulation.

Used in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction due to mortality benefit demonstrated in clinical trials, unlike some earlier beta-blockers without proven survival advantage in HFrEF.

## Primary Uses

- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (chronic, guideline-directed medical therapy)
- Left ventricular dysfunction post-myocardial infarction
- Hypertension
- Angina pectoris (selected patients)

Extended-release once-daily formulation improves adherence for hypertension and heart failure.

## Common Side Effects

- Dizziness, hypotension (especially initiation)
- Bradycardia
- Fatigue
- Weight gain (fluid retention early in heart failure titration)
- Diarrhea
- Impaired glucose masking of hypoglycemia symptoms in diabetes
- Bronchospasm in reactive airway disease (less cardioselective than metoprolol succinate for asthma risk, but still caution)
- **Warnings:** Do not abruptly discontinue in coronary disease; start low and titrate in heart failure; may worsen heart failure transiently before benefit; contraindicated in decompensated heart failure requiring IV inotropes, high-grade block, and severe bradycardia.
