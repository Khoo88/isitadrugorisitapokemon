## What is Lisinopril?

Lisinopril is an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor that blocks conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II, reducing vasoconstriction, aldosterone secretion, and sympathetic activation. It decreases blood pressure, afterload, and maladaptive remodeling in heart failure and post-myocardial infarction.

Unlike enalapril and ramipril, lisinopril is not a prodrug and does not require hepatic activation. Renal elimination requires dose adjustment in kidney disease. ACE inhibitors increase bradykinin, causing dry cough in 5–20% of patients and rare angioedema.

They are cornerstone therapy in hypertension, heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, and diabetic nephropathy, with proven mortality benefit in selected populations.

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (with beta-blocker and other GDMT)
- Post-myocardial infarction (LV dysfunction or heart failure)
- Diabetic nephropathy with proteinuria (type 1 and type 2 diabetes)
- Chronic kidney disease with proteinuria (selected patients)

Avoid in pregnancy and bilateral renal artery stenosis.

## Common Side Effects

- Dry cough
- Hyperkalemia
- Dizziness, hypotension (especially first dose)
- Elevated creatinine (hemodynamic reduction in GFR—expected to some degree)
- Angioedema (rare but life-threatening)
- Rash
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated in pregnancy; monitor potassium and creatinine after initiation; hold before surgery per protocol; do not combine with aliskiren in diabetes; swelling of lips/tongue requires emergency care.
