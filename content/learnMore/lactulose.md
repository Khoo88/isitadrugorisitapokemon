## What is Lactulose?

Lactulose is a synthetic disaccharide osmotic laxative that is not absorbed in the small intestine and reaches the colon intact, where gut bacteria metabolize it to lactic, acetic, and formic acids. Acidification traps ammonia as ammonium ion, lowering colonic pH and plasma ammonia in hepatic encephalopathy while osmotic effects soften stool.

Onset for constipation is 24–48 hours; titration to soft stools is standard. Flatulence and bloating are common initially. It is safe in pregnancy for constipation when stimulants are undesirable.

Excessive dosing causes diarrhea, dehydration, and electrolyte imbalance; patients with hepatic encephalopathy require careful titration to 2–3 soft stools daily.

## Primary Uses

- Constipation (chronic, including opioid-induced bowel regimen component)
- Hepatic encephalopathy (prevention and treatment of hyperammonemia)
- Bowel preparation adjunct (selected protocols)

Preferred osmotic agent for hepatic encephalopathy over many alternatives when oral route is feasible.

## Common Side Effects

- Flatulence, bloating, abdominal cramping
- Diarrhea (dose-related)
- Dehydration and electrolyte disturbances with overdose
- Nausea
- **Warnings:** Avoid in galactosemia; caution in diabetes (contains galactose/fructose metabolites, minimal systemic glucose effect but caloric content); patients with ileostomy or inadequate fluid intake risk dehydration.
