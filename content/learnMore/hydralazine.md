## What is Hydralazine?

Hydralazine is a direct-acting arterial vasodilator that relaxes vascular smooth muscle through unclear mechanisms involving cyclic GMP and calcium handling, reducing systemic vascular resistance and blood pressure. Reflex tachycardia and sodium retention often occur unless combined with beta-blockers and diuretics.

It is metabolized by acetylation (fast vs. slow acetylator phenotypes affect levels). A lupus-like syndrome can develop with prolonged high-dose therapy, especially in slow acetylators, featuring arthralgia, rash, and positive antinuclear antibodies.

Still used in pregnancy for hypertensive emergencies and chronic hypertension when safer profile for fetus is needed (labetalol and methyldopa also common).

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension (often combination therapy—not first-line monotherapy)
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (combined with isosorbide dinitrate—BiDil indication in self-identified Black patients; used more broadly off-label in some centers)
- Hypertensive emergencies in pregnancy (IV, per protocol)

## Common Side Effects

- Reflex tachycardia, palpitations
- Headache
- Fluid retention, edema
- Drug-induced lupus erythematosus-like syndrome
- Peripheral neuropathy (high dose, prolonged use)
- Nausea, diarrhea
- **Warnings:** Monitor ANA and symptoms with chronic therapy; combine with beta-blocker to blunt tachycardia; contraindicated in coronary artery disease as monotherapy (coronary steal concern); hypotension with IV use.
