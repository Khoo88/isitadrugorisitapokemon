## What is Allopurinol?

Allopurinol is a xanthine oxidase inhibitor that blocks the conversion of hypoxanthine and xanthine to uric acid, the terminal step in purine catabolism. By lowering serum uric acid production, it reduces supersaturation of urate in joints and kidneys, preventing crystal formation and promoting dissolution of existing urate deposits over time.

Allopurinol and its active metabolite oxypurinol are renally eliminated; dose reduction is required in chronic kidney disease. Initiation can transiently alter urate pools and trigger gout flares; colchicine or NSAID prophylaxis is often co-prescribed during the first months of urate-lowering therapy.

A severe hypersensitivity syndrome (allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome) occurs rarely but carries significant morbidity, especially in certain genetic backgrounds (e.g., HLA-B*58:01 in some Asian and African populations). Screening recommendations vary by guideline and region.

## Primary Uses

- Chronic management of hyperuricemia in gout
- Prevention of urate nephropathy in malignancy with tumor lysis risk (often with rasburicase or hydration protocols)
- Calcium oxalate kidney stones with hyperuricosuria (selected patients)
- Recurrent uric acid kidney stones

It is not indicated for acute gout flares alone and may worsen flares if started without anti-inflammatory prophylaxis.

## Common Side Effects

- Rash (maculopapular—can progress to severe cutaneous adverse reactions)
- Gastrointestinal upset (nausea, diarrhea)
- Elevated liver transaminases
- Acute gout flares during initiation (urate mobilization)
- Allopurinol hypersensitivity syndrome (fever, eosinophilia, hepatitis, renal failure, Stevens-Johnson/toxic epidermal necrolysis spectrum)
- **Warnings:** Genetic testing for HLA-B*58:01 considered in at-risk populations; start low and titrate to target serum urate; adjust dose in renal impairment.
