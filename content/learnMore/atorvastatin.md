## What is Atorvastatin?

Atorvastatin is a hydroxymethylglutaryl-coenzyme A (HMG-CoA) reductase inhibitor (statin) that blocks the rate-limiting step in hepatic cholesterol synthesis. Reduced intracellular cholesterol upregulates LDL receptors on hepatocytes, increasing clearance of low-density lipoprotein (LDL) from blood.

Atorvastatin is among the more potent statins, capable of substantial LDL reduction at standard doses and used in high-intensity regimens for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease risk reduction. It is metabolized by CYP3A4; grapefruit juice and strong CYP3A4 inhibitors increase exposure and myopathy risk.

Beyond lipid lowering, statins exhibit pleiotropic effects on endothelial function and inflammation, though clinical benefit is primarily attributed to LDL reduction.

## Primary Uses

- Primary and secondary prevention of atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD)
- Hyperlipidemia and familial hypercholesterolemia (heterozygous)
- Adjunct to diet in adults and children (selected ages) with elevated LDL

High-intensity dosing (e.g., 40–80 mg) is recommended for secondary prevention in many patients with established ASCVD, per guideline LDL targets.

## Common Side Effects

- Myalgia, muscle weakness
- Rhabdomyolysis (rare but serious)
- Elevated hepatic transaminases (usually reversible)
- Gastrointestinal symptoms (constipation, diarrhea, dyspepsia)
- Headache
- New-onset diabetes mellitus (small increased risk with long-term statin use)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in active liver disease; assess muscle symptoms; drug interactions with CYP3A4 inhibitors; discontinue if marked CK elevation with symptoms; pregnancy contraindicated.
