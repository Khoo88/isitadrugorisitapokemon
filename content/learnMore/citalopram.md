## What is Citalopram?

Citalopram is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) that blocks the serotonin transporter (SERT) on presynaptic neurons, increasing synaptic serotonin and desensitizing inhibitory autoreceptors over time. It has minimal effect on norepinephrine and dopamine reuptake at therapeutic doses.

The S-enantiomer (escitalopram) contributes most serotonin reuptake inhibition; citalopram is a racemic mixture. Hepatic metabolism via CYP2C19 and CYP3A4 produces metabolites including desmethylcitalopram.

FDA recommends maximum 40 mg daily (20 mg in adults over 60) due to dose-dependent QT prolongation on electrocardiogram.

## Primary Uses

- Major depressive disorder
- Generalized anxiety disorder (approved in some regions)
- Panic disorder
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder (often higher doses, per guidelines)
- Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (continuous or luteal-phase dosing)

First-line antidepressant class alongside other SSRIs and SNRIs; full response may require 4–8 weeks.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, diarrhea, dry mouth
- Sexual dysfunction (decreased libido, anorgasmia, erectile dysfunction)
- Insomnia or somnolence
- Weight change
- Headache
- QT prolongation at high doses
- Serotonin syndrome (with MAO inhibitors, other serotonergic drugs)
- Increased suicidal ideation in young adults (class boxed warning, early treatment monitoring)
- **Warnings:** Do not combine with MAOIs; taper to discontinue; hyponatremia (SIADH) in elderly; bleeding risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants.
