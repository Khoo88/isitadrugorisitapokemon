## What is Albuterol?

Albuterol (salbutamol) is a selective short-acting β2-adrenergic receptor agonist (SABA). It relaxes bronchial smooth muscle by stimulating β2 receptors, increasing intracellular cyclic AMP, which activates protein kinase A and reduces calcium availability for contraction. This produces rapid bronchodilation within minutes when inhaled.

Inhaled delivery maximizes pulmonary effect while limiting systemic β2 stimulation compared with oral routes. Systemic absorption still occurs and can cause tachycardia and tremor at higher doses. Albuterol is the cornerstone rescue medication for acute bronchospasm in asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).

Tolerance to bronchodilator effect can develop with excessive scheduled use; inflammation control with inhaled corticosteroids remains essential in persistent asthma.

## Primary Uses

- Relief of acute bronchospasm in asthma
- Prevention of exercise-induced bronchospasm
- Acute symptom relief in COPD
- Adjunct bronchodilation in acute bronchospasm from other causes (allergic reaction, viral bronchiolitis in age-appropriate settings per guidelines)
- Nebulized or metered-dose inhaler use in emergency and outpatient settings

It is not a substitute for controller therapy in persistent asthma; overreliance on SABA alone is associated with increased exacerbation risk.

## Common Side Effects

- Tremor (especially hands)
- Tachycardia, palpitations
- Nervousness, restlessness
- Headache
- Muscle cramps (with diuretic co-therapy and hypokalemia)
- Paradoxical bronchospasm (rare—requires discontinuation and alternative therapy)
- Hypokalemia with high or frequent doses
- **Warnings:** Use with caution in cardiovascular disease, hyperthyroidism, and diabetes; monitor for deteriorating asthma control when rescue use increases.
