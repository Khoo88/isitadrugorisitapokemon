## What is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist with 94% homology to native GLP-1, modified for resistance to dipeptidyl peptidase-4 degradation and albumin binding for prolonged half-life. Subcutaneous weekly and oral daily formulations stimulate glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppress glucagon, slow gastric emptying, and reduce appetite via central pathways.

Significant weight loss and cardiovascular risk reduction (SELECT trial for obesity; SUSTAIN/SMART for diabetes) have expanded indications. It is not insulin and has low intrinsic hypoglycemia risk unless combined with insulin or sulfonylureas.

Gastrointestinal side effects are common initially; dose escalation mitigates intolerance. Thyroid C-cell tumor signal exists in rodents; medullary thyroid carcinoma and MEN2 are contraindications.

## Primary Uses

- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (glycemic control and cardiovascular risk reduction)
- Chronic weight management in obesity or overweight with comorbidities (higher-dose Wegovy labeling)
- Reduction of major adverse cardiovascular events in obesity with established cardiovascular disease (SELECT indication)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation
- Decreased appetite
- Abdominal distension, reflux
- Injection-site reactions
- Gallbladder disease (cholelithiasis)
- Pancreatitis (rare, causal link debated)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated in personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2; hold before surgery per protocols; delayed gastric emptying affects oral medication absorption; avoid in pregnancy; diabetic retinopathy worsening reported with rapid glucose improvement.
