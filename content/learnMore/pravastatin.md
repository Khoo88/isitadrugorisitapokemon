## What is Pravastatin?

Pravastatin is a hydrophilic HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) that blocks hepatic cholesterol synthesis, upregulating LDL receptors and lowering low-density lipoprotein cholesterol. Hydrophilicity results in less hepatic uptake via OATP transporters and fewer CYP-mediated drug interactions than lipophilic statins (atorvastatin, simvastatin).

It is among the less potent statins at standard doses but has established cardiovascular benefit in primary and secondary prevention trials. Hepatotoxicity and myopathy are class effects; pravastatin may have lower myopathy risk when combined with certain drugs due to minimal CYP3A4 metabolism.

Active transport and renal excretion contribute to elimination; dose adjustment is generally not required for mild renal impairment.

## Primary Uses

- Primary hyperlipidemia and mixed dyslipidemia
- Primary prevention of cardiovascular events in at-risk patients
- Secondary prevention post-myocardial infarction or stroke
- Post-transplant hyperlipidemia (when drug interaction profile favors pravastatin over simvastatin)

Often chosen when statin-drug interaction risk is high (HIV protease inhibitors, cyclosporine caution still applies).

## Common Side Effects

- Myalgia, muscle weakness
- Rhabdomyolysis (rare)
- Elevated hepatic transaminases
- Headache, gastrointestinal upset
- **Warnings:** Avoid in active liver disease; assess muscle symptoms; pregnancy contraindicated; lower potency may require higher dose or switch for aggressive LDL targets.
