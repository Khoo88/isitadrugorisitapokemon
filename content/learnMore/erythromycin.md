## What is Erythromycin?

Erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic that binds the 50S ribosomal subunit, inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis. It is bacteriostatic or bactericidal depending on concentration and organism. Activity includes gram-positive cocci, some gram-negatives, and atypical pathogens (Mycoplasma, Legionella, Chlamydia).

Gastrointestinal motilin agonist activity stimulates gastric emptying (used for gastroparesis in diabetic patients). It inhibits CYP3A4, raising levels of many co-administered drugs (statins, warfarin, benzodiazepines).

QT prolongation is a class effect; erythromycin carries higher GI side effect burden than azithromycin.

## Primary Uses

- Alternative for penicillin-allergic patients with streptococcal infections (resistance limits empiric use)
- Legionella pneumonia
- Pertussis (whooping cough)
- Acne (topical)
- Diabetic gastroparesis (oral, erythromycin base—prokinetic use)
- Chlamydia in pregnancy (when azithromycin unsuitable, per guidelines)
- Preoperative bowel prep component (historical)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramping (very common orally)
- Diarrhea
- QT prolongation, arrhythmia risk
- Hepatotoxicity (cholestatic, especially estolate salt)
- Ototoxicity (high IV doses)
- **Warnings:** Major CYP3A4 interactions; avoid with simvastatin/lovastatin; caution in QT prolongation; C. difficile colitis; resistance common in Streptococcus pneumoniae limiting empiric respiratory use.
