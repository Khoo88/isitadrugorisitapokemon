## What is Prednisone?

Prednisone is a synthetic glucocorticoid prodrug converted hepatically by 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase to active prednisolone. It suppresses inflammation and immune responses through glucocorticoid receptor–mediated gene regulation, inhibiting phospholipase A2, cytokines, and leukocyte trafficking.

Intermediate duration of action suits once-daily or alternate-day dosing for chronic inflammatory conditions. Mineralocorticoid activity is modest at anti-inflammatory doses but contributes to fluid retention at higher doses.

Abrupt discontinuation after prolonged use risks adrenal crisis; stress-dose steroids are required during illness or surgery in suppressed patients.

## Primary Uses

- Wide-ranging inflammatory and autoimmune diseases (RA, lupus, vasculitis, sarcoidosis)
- Asthma and COPD exacerbations
- Allergic reactions (severe)
- Inflammatory bowel disease
- Pneumonia with significant hypoxemia (short course, evidence-based protocols in COVID-19 and CAP)
- Organ transplant rejection (with other immunosuppressants)
- Cancer chemotherapy adjunct (lymphoid malignancies, antiemetic protocols)

Lowest effective dose for shortest duration is standard practice.

## Common Side Effects

- Hyperglycemia, weight gain, cushingoid habitus
- Mood disturbance, insomnia
- Hypertension, edema
- Osteoporosis, cataracts, glaucoma
- Immunosuppression and infection
- Peptic ulcer risk (with NSAIDs)
- Adrenal suppression
- **Warnings:** Taper protocols after chronic use; live vaccine contraindication; monitor glucose and bone health with long-term therapy.
