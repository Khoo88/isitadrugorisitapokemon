## What is Isosorbide?

Isosorbide refers to organic nitrate vasodilators—principally isosorbide mononitrate and isosorbide dinitrate—that donate nitric oxide in vascular smooth muscle, activating guanylate cyclase, increasing cyclic GMP, and causing venodilation (and some arterial dilation). Venous pooling reduces preload and myocardial oxygen demand in angina; arterial dilation lowers afterload.

Continuous around-the-clock dosing of short-acting formulations leads to tolerance (tachyphylaxis); asymmetric or intermittent dosing preserves efficacy. Long-acting mononitrate once daily has more predictable pharmacokinetics than dinitrate.

Headache is common initially and often diminishes. Hypotension and syncope occur with excessive vasodilation or combination with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors.

## Primary Uses

- Chronic stable angina prophylaxis (mononitrate or dinitrate regimens)
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (isosorbide dinitrate with hydralazine—BiDil and general use)
- Acute angina (sublingual nitroglycerin preferred for immediate relief; oral isosorbide not for acute attack)

## Common Side Effects

- Headache (very common initially)
- Hypotension, dizziness, syncope
- Flushing
- Tolerance with improper continuous dosing schedules
- Reflex tachycardia
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated with PDE5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil) within 24–48 hours—severe hypotension; caution in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with outflow obstruction; taper extended use to avoid rebound angina.
