## What is Nitrofurantoin?

Nitrofurantoin is a nitrofuran antibacterial agent whose active metabolites damage bacterial DNA and ribosomal proteins. It achieves high urinary concentrations with minimal systemic levels, making it ideal for uncomplicated lower urinary tract infections while limiting systemic toxicity.

It is ineffective for pyelonephritis and systemic infections due to poor tissue penetration. Resistance rates vary; empiric use should consider local susceptibility and prior use.

Contraindicated when creatinine clearance is very low (reduced urinary excretion and increased systemic toxicity risk per labeling, typically CrCl <30 mL/min).

## Primary Uses

- Uncomplicated urinary tract infection (cystitis) in women
- Urinary tract infection prophylaxis (recurrent cystitis, nightly low dose)
- UTI in pregnancy (selected first-line agent per guidelines when susceptible)

Not for upper tract infection, prostatitis, or perinephric abscess.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, vomiting (take with food to reduce)
- Headache, dizziness
- Pulmonary toxicity (acute, subacute, or chronic—with long-term use)
- Peripheral neuropathy (especially renal impairment or prolonged use)
- Hemolytic anemia in G6PD deficiency
- **Warnings:** Avoid in CrCl <30 mL/min; avoid in term pregnancy near delivery (neonatal hemolysis risk per some guidelines); discontinue if pulmonary symptoms develop; not for renal parenchymal infection.
