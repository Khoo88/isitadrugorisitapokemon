## What is Rifampin?

Rifampin (rifampicin) is a rifamycin antibiotic that inhibits bacterial DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, blocking transcription. It is bactericidal against Mycobacterium tuberculosis and has activity against many gram-positive organisms and some gram-negatives. It penetrates tissues including CSF and macrophages.

Potent induction of hepatic CYP enzymes and P-glycoprotein causes clinically significant drug interactions (oral contraceptives, warfarin, HIV antiretrovirals, immunosuppressants). Body fluids may turn orange-red (tears, urine, sweat)—patients should be counseled.

Hepatotoxicity, often in combination with isoniazid and pyrazinamide, requires monitoring in TB regimens.

## Primary Uses

- Tuberculosis (intensive and continuation phases, always in combination)
- Latent tuberculosis infection (alternative regimens, e.g., 4-month rifampin monotherapy per guidelines)
- Mycobacterium avium complex (combination therapy)
- Staphylococcal prosthetic device infections (combination with beta-lactams, biofilm indication)
- Asymptomatic meningococcal carrier prophylaxis (outbreak settings)
- Brucellosis, leprosy (combination regimens)

## Common Side Effects

- Hepatotoxicity
- Orange discoloration of body fluids
- Nausea, anorexia
- Flu-like syndrome (intermittent high-dose use)
- Thrombocytopenia, hemolytic anemia (intermittent)
- **Warnings:** Major drug interactions—review all medications; non-hormonal contraception during therapy; monitor liver enzymes; may reduce efficacy of many co-medications; resistance develops rapidly if used alone.
