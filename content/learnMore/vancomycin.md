## What is Vancomycin?

Vancomycin is a glycopeptide antibiotic that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding D-alanyl-D-alanine termini of peptidoglycan precursors, blocking transglycosylation and transpeptidation. It is bactericidal against most gram-positive organisms, including methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and many enterococci (VRE requires combination for bactericidal effect).

Poor oral absorption limits oral use to Clostridioides difficile colitis (acts locally in gut). IV therapy requires therapeutic drug monitoring (trough or AUC/MIC targets) to optimize efficacy and minimize nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity.

"Red man syndrome" is an infusion-related histamine reaction slowed by appropriate infusion rates.

## Primary Uses

- Serious MRSA infections (bacteremia, pneumonia, endocarditis, osteomyelitis)
- Coagulase-negative staphylococcal device infections
- C. difficile colitis (oral)
- Surgical prophylaxis for beta-lactam allergy in high-risk procedures
- Empiric gram-positive coverage in sepsis and meningitis (with other agents)

## Common Side Effects

- Nephrotoxicity (especially with high troughs, aminoglycosides, contrast)
- Ototoxicity (vestibular and cochlear)
- Red man syndrome (flushing, hypotension—rate-related)
- Thrombophlebitis at infusion site
- Neutropenia (prolonged use)
- **Warnings:** TDM mandatory for IV; AUC-guided dosing preferred in many centers; avoid rapid infusion; monitor renal function; VRE and VISA/VRSA emerging resistance.
