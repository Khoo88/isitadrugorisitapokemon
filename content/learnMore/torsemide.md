## What is Torsemide?

Torsemide is a loop diuretic structurally related to furosemide that inhibits the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, producing potent diuresis and natriuresis. It has more predictable oral bioavailability than furosemide (80–100% vs. variable) and a longer duration of action, allowing once- or twice-daily dosing.

Hepatic metabolism via CYP2C9 produces active metabolites; renal excretion also occurs. Used in heart failure, cirrhosis, and nephrotic syndrome when loop diuresis is required.

Electrolyte losses (potassium, magnesium) and ototoxicity are class effects; monitoring is essential.

## Primary Uses

- Edema associated with heart failure, renal disease, or hepatic disease
- Hypertension (adjunct when thiazides insufficient)
- Acute fluid overload (IV formulation)

## Common Side Effects

- Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia
- Hypotension, dehydration, renal dysfunction if over-diuresed
- Ototoxicity (high IV doses)
- Gout flares (hyperuricemia)
- **Warnings:** Monitor electrolytes and renal function; ototoxicity with aminoglycosides; allergy to sulfa drugs—cross-reactivity possible; avoid in anuria.
