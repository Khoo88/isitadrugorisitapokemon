## What is Docusate?

Docusate sodium (dioctyl sulfosuccinate) is an anionic surfactant stool softener that reduces surface tension of stool, allowing water and lipids to penetrate and soften fecal matter. It acts as a wetting agent in the bowel lumen with minimal systemic absorption.

Efficacy for established constipation is modest compared with osmotic laxatives; it is commonly combined with stimulant laxatives in hospital bowel regimens and opioid-induced constipation protocols. Adequate fluid intake improves effectiveness.

It does not stimulate colonic motility and is not appropriate as sole therapy for fecal impaction without disimpaction and other agents.

## Primary Uses

- Stool softening in constipation (often adjunct)
- Prevention of straining after surgery or myocardial infarction (per historical protocols; evidence mixed)
- Opioid bowel regimens (with stimulant laxative such as senna)
- Ear wax softening (cerumenolytic topical use in some products)

## Common Side Effects

- Generally mild
- Abdominal cramping (uncommon)
- Diarrhea with excessive use
- Throat irritation if liquid formulation used incorrectly (aspiration risk—use with caution)
- **Warnings:** Not for use as sole agent in opioid constipation without stimulant laxative; mineral oil absorption theoretically increased if co-administered (avoid combination); limited evidence as standalone therapy for chronic constipation.
