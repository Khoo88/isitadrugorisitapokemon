## What is Olanzapine?

Olanzapine is an atypical (second-generation) antipsychotic with antagonism at dopamine D2, serotonin 5-HT2A, histamine H1, and muscarinic M1 receptors. Broad receptor profile contributes to efficacy in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder but also to metabolic and sedative adverse effects.

It is highly effective for positive and negative symptoms of schizophrenia and acute mania (oral and intramuscular). Long-acting injectable depot formulation supports adherence.

Metabolism via CYP1A2 (smoking induces, fluvoxamine inhibits) and glucuronidation. Weight gain and dyslipidemia are among the most pronounced in the class.

## Primary Uses

- Schizophrenia (acute and maintenance)
- Bipolar I disorder (acute mania, mixed episodes, maintenance)
- Agitation associated with schizophrenia or bipolar disorder (IM)
- Treatment-resistant depression (adjunct with fluoxetine—combination product)
- Chemotherapy-associated nausea (off-label, specialist use)

## Common Side Effects

- Weight gain, hyperglycemia, dyslipidemia (metabolic syndrome risk)
- Sedation, somnolence
- Increased appetite
- Extrapyramidal symptoms (lower than haloperidol but present)
- Anticholinergic effects (constipation, dry mouth)
- Orthostatic hypotension
- **Warnings:** Increased mortality in elderly with dementia-related psychosis (boxed warning); monitor metabolic parameters; neuroleptic malignant syndrome; avoid in severe hepatic impairment without caution.
