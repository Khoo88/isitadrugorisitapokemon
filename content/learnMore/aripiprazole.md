## What is Aripiprazole?

Aripiprazole is an atypical antipsychotic classified as a dopamine system stabilizer. It acts as a partial agonist at dopamine D2 and D3 receptors and serotonin 5-HT1A receptors, and as an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors. This profile differs from typical antipsychotics (D2 antagonism) and some atypicals with stronger histaminic and muscarinic blockade.

Partial agonism at D2 receptors is proposed to reduce excessive dopamine in mesolimbic pathways (positive symptoms) while supporting dopamine tone in mesocortical pathways (cognitive and negative symptoms). Metabolism is primarily via CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 to active metabolite dehydro-aripiprazole.

Long-acting injectable formulations provide sustained plasma levels for maintenance treatment in schizophrenia and bipolar disorder.

## Primary Uses

- Schizophrenia (acute and maintenance)
- Bipolar I disorder (acute manic/mixed episodes and maintenance monotherapy or adjunct)
- Major depressive disorder (adjunctive therapy to antidepressants)
- Irritability associated with autistic disorder (pediatric)
- Tourette disorder (pediatric, selected cases)

Used when metabolic and extrapyramidal profiles favor partial agonism, though individual response varies.

## Common Side Effects

- Akathisia (restless inner tension—dose-related)
- Insomnia, anxiety, agitation
- Headache
- Nausea, constipation
- Weight gain (generally less than olanzapine or clozapine but still occurs)
- Extrapyramidal symptoms (less than typical antipsychotics)
- **Warnings:** Increased mortality in elderly with dementia-related psychosis (class warning); metabolic monitoring; impulse-control disorders reported (gambling, binge eating); neuroleptic malignant syndrome (rare).
