## What is Loperamide?

Loperamide is a peripherally acting mu-opioid receptor agonist that inhibits acetylcholine and prostaglandin release in the intestinal wall, slowing motility and increasing fluid absorption without significant central nervous system penetration at therapeutic doses. It increases stool transit time and anal sphincter tone.

Available over the counter for acute diarrhea; higher prescription doses exist for chronic diarrhea and ileostomy output. Abuse at very high doses can produce opioid central effects and cardiac arrhythmias (QT prolongation), leading to FDA packaging limits and public health warnings.

It does not treat the underlying cause of infectious diarrhea and may worsen invasive bacterial colitis if used inappropriately.

## Primary Uses

- Acute nonspecific diarrhea symptomatic relief
- Chronic diarrhea (prescription dosing, specialist guidance)
- Reduction of ileostomy output (adjunct)
- Traveler’s diarrhea symptomatic relief (after assessing for dysentery or fever)

Oral rehydration remains essential; antibiotics only for specific pathogens.

## Common Side Effects

- Constipation (if overused)
- Abdominal cramping, bloating
- Dizziness (uncommon at standard doses)
- Nausea
- **Warnings:** Avoid in bloody diarrhea, high fever, or suspected invasive bacterial infection; do not exceed recommended OTC doses; cardiac toxicity with massive overdose; caution with drugs that prolong QT when misused; opioid withdrawal does not apply at therapeutic doses.
