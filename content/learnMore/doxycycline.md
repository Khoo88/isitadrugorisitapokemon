## What is Doxycycline?

Doxycycline is a tetracycline antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 30S ribosomal subunit. It is bacteriostatic against a broad range of gram-positive, gram-negative, atypical (Mycoplasma, Chlamydia, Rickettsia), and many anaerobic organisms.

Lipophilicity and long half-life permit once- or twice-daily dosing. Absorption is decreased by calcium, iron, and antacids. Unlike older tetracyclines, doxycycline has less effect on calcium deposition in teeth when used in children beyond age 8 per current guidelines for short courses.

Photosensitivity is a class effect; sun protection is advised during therapy.

## Primary Uses

- Community-acquired pneumonia (atypical coverage)
- Skin infections including acne and rosacea
- Tick-borne illnesses (Lyme disease, Rocky Mountain spotted fever, ehrlichiosis)
- Chlamydia and pelvic inflammatory disease (combination regimens)
- Malaria prophylaxis and treatment (combination)
- Periodontitis (adjunct)
- Anthrax (post-exposure, alternative)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, esophageal irritation (take upright with water)
- Photosensitivity rash
- Diarrhea
- Clostridioides difficile colitis
- Vestibular toxicity (dizziness—more with minocycline)
- Tooth discoloration and enamel hypoplasia in young children (avoid prolonged use when possible)
- Hepatotoxicity (rare, including with high-dose IV)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in pregnancy (fetal bone/teeth effects); do not combine with isotretinoin; warfarin interaction; contraceptive failure debated—backup advised by some guidelines.
