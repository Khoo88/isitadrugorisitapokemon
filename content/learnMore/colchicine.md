## What is Colchicine?

Colchicine is an alkaloid that disrupts microtubule polymerization by binding tubulin, impairing leukocyte migration and phagocytosis in inflamed joints. In gout, it reduces inflammatory response to deposited monosodium urate crystals rather than lowering uric acid directly.

Low-dose regimens for acute gout and prophylaxis during urate-lowering therapy have improved safety versus historic high-dose protocols that caused severe diarrhea and toxicity. It is metabolized by CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein; strong inhibitors increase toxicity risk.

Colchicine also has roles in familial Mediterranean fever and pericarditis per evidence-based dosing protocols.

## Primary Uses

- Acute gout flares (low-dose regimen when renal function permits)
- Prophylaxis of gout flares when initiating allopurinol or febuxostat
- Familial Mediterranean fever
- Recurrent pericarditis (adjunct, per cardiology guidelines)

Not a substitute for long-term urate-lowering therapy in chronic gout management.

## Common Side Effects

- Diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramping
- Myelosuppression and neuromyopathy (overdose or drug interactions)
- Elevated liver enzymes (uncommon)
- Alopecia (rare)
- **Warnings:** Avoid strong CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors (clarithromycin, cyclosporine, many azoles) without dose adjustment; reduce dose in renal/hepatic impairment; toxicity can be fatal in overdose; no antidote—supportive care.
