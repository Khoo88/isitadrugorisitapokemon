## What is Ciprofloxacin?

Ciprofloxacin is a fluoroquinolone antibiotic that inhibits bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, preventing DNA replication and transcription. It is bactericidal with broad gram-negative activity, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa in many strains, and variable gram-positive coverage.

Oral and intravenous formulations achieve high tissue penetration, including in the urinary tract, prostate, and lungs. Resistance has increased with widespread use; empiric prescribing should follow local antibiograms.

Fluoroquinolones carry FDA warnings regarding disabling and potentially permanent serious adverse reactions affecting tendons, muscles, joints, nerves, and central nervous system, limiting use to situations where alternatives are not appropriate.

## Primary Uses

- Complicated urinary tract infections and pyelonephritis (when alternatives unsuitable)
- Acute infectious diarrhea (selected travelers, bacterial)
- Chronic bacterial prostatitis
- Bone and joint infections (combination therapy, culture-directed)
- Anthrax post-exposure prophylaxis and treatment
- Pseudomonal infections in cystic fibrosis (specialist protocols)
- Intra-abdominal infections (combination regimens, when indicated)

Avoided for uncomplicated cystitis in many regions when other agents suffice.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, diarrhea
- Headache, dizziness
- Tendinitis, tendon rupture (especially Achilles, increased with corticosteroids)
- Peripheral neuropathy
- QT prolongation
- Photosensitivity
- CNS effects (confusion, seizures in predisposed patients)
- Clostridioides difficile colitis
- **Warnings:** FDA boxed warnings for serious adverse effects; avoid in myasthenia gravis; reserve for indicated infections; drug interactions (theophylline, warfarin, antacids reducing absorption).
