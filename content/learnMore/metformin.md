## What is Metformin?

Metformin is a biguanide antihyperglycemic that lowers blood glucose primarily by decreasing hepatic glucose production (gluconeogenesis) and modestly improving insulin sensitivity in peripheral tissues. It does not stimulate insulin secretion, so hypoglycemia risk as monotherapy is low.

It activates AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) pathways, influencing lipid metabolism and possibly cardiovascular outcomes. Metformin does not cause weight gain and often promotes modest weight loss. Renal elimination as unchanged drug requires dose adjustment or discontinuation when estimated GFR falls below guideline thresholds to reduce lactic acidosis risk.

Extended-release formulations improve gastrointestinal tolerability. It is first-line pharmacotherapy for type 2 diabetes per major guidelines and is used in prediabetes and polycystic ovary syndrome.

## Primary Uses

- Type 2 diabetes mellitus (first-line unless contraindicated)
- Prediabetes (diabetes prevention in high-risk individuals)
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (insulin resistance, off-label/common)
- Gestational diabetes (selected cases, specialist use)
- Adjunct in type 1 diabetes for insulin resistance (off-label)

Often combined with SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists, or other agents for cardiovascular and renal benefit.

## Common Side Effects

- Gastrointestinal upset (nausea, diarrhea, metallic taste—often transient)
- Vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use
- Lactic acidosis (rare, associated with renal failure, hypoxia, excessive alcohol)
- **Warnings:** Hold before iodinated contrast and surgery per protocols; contraindicated in eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m² (use varies for 30–45); avoid excessive alcohol; B12 monitoring with chronic therapy.
