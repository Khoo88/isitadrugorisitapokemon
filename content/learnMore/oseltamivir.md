## What is Oseltamivir?

Oseltamivir is an oral neuraminidase inhibitor antiviral that blocks the influenza neuraminidase enzyme, preventing release of new virions from infected host cells and limiting spread within the respiratory tract. It is a prodrug converted hepatically to the active metabolite oseltamivir carboxylate.

Treatment is most effective when started within 48 hours of symptom onset; benefit in uncomplicated influenza includes modest reduction in duration of illness by approximately one day. It does not replace annual influenza vaccination and resistance can emerge with widespread use.

Renal elimination of the active metabolite requires dose adjustment in renal impairment. Inhaled zanamivir and IV peramivir are alternative neuraminidase inhibitors with different routes and contraindications.

## Primary Uses

- Treatment of acute uncomplicated influenza in patients at higher risk of complications (when started early)
- Treatment of influenza in hospitalized patients (per institutional and CDC guidance)
- Post-exposure prophylaxis for close contacts in outbreak settings or high-risk individuals
- Seasonal prophylaxis during community outbreaks (selected high-risk populations)

Not effective against non-influenza respiratory viruses (common cold, COVID-19, RSV).

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, vomiting (often transient, reduced with food)
- Headache
- Neuropsychiatric events (rare reports in pediatric patients—confusion, self-injury; causality debated)
- **Warnings:** Not a substitute for vaccination; adjust dose in renal failure; limited benefit if started >48 hours after onset in low-risk outpatients per many guidelines; bronchospasm risk with inhaled alternatives does not apply to oral oseltamivir.
