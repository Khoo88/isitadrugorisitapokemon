## What is Tramadol?

Tramadol is a centrally acting analgesic with weak mu-opioid agonism and inhibition of norepinephrine and serotonin reuptake, contributing to analgesia in moderate pain. Active metabolite O-desmethyltramadol (M1) is produced via CYP2D6 and is more potent at mu receptors—poor metabolizers have reduced efficacy, ultrarapid metabolizers increased toxicity risk.

Lower abuse potential than morphine historically led to less restrictive scheduling in some regions, though opioid crisis experience increased scrutiny. Seizure risk increases with serotonergic drugs and at supratherapeutic doses.

Also available in combination with acetaminophen. Schedule IV controlled substance in the United States.

## Primary Uses

- Moderate to moderately severe pain (acute and chronic, when opioid appropriate)
- Chronic pain conditions (osteoarthritis, neuropathic pain—variable evidence)

Not first-line for chronic non-cancer pain per CDC opioid guidelines; non-opioid multimodal therapy preferred.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, dizziness, constipation
- Somnolence, headache
- Seizures (dose-related, interaction-related)
- Serotonin syndrome (with SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs)
- Respiratory depression (especially with other CNS depressants)
- **Warnings:** CYP2D6 genetic variability; avoid with MAOIs; reduce dose in renal/hepatic impairment; addiction and misuse potential; do not combine with benzodiazepines without extreme caution.
