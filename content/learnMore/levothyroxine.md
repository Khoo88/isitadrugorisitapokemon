## What is Levothyroxine?

Levothyroxine is synthetic thyroxine (T4), identical to endogenous hormone, that replaces deficient thyroid hormone in hypothyroidism and suppresses TSH in thyroid cancer management. T4 is converted peripherally to triiodothyronine (T3) by deiodinases; levothyroxine provides steady prohormone for physiological T3 production.

Absorption is reduced by food, coffee, calcium, iron, and proton pump inhibitors; consistent fasting administration improves consistency. Half-life is approximately one week, so steady state requires 4–6 weeks after dose changes; TSH monitoring guides titration.

Overreplacement causes thyrotoxicosis (arrhythmia, bone loss, anxiety); underreplacement leaves hypothyroid symptoms and metabolic effects.

## Primary Uses

- Primary, secondary, and tertiary hypothyroidism
- Thyroid hormone suppression after thyroid cancer treatment
- Goiter and thyroid nodule TSH suppression (selected cases)
- Congenital hypothyroidism (neonatal screening and lifelong therapy)

Not for treatment of obesity or male infertility without hypothyroidism.

## Common Side Effects

**When appropriately dosed:** generally none

**Overdose/excessive dose:**
- Palpitations, tachycardia, atrial fibrillation
- Weight loss, heat intolerance, tremor
- Osteoporosis with chronic excess
- **Warnings:** Do not use for weight loss; titrate slowly in elderly and cardiac disease; many drug interactions (warfarin, digoxin, calcium, PPIs); pregnancy requires dose increases often; brand/generic switching may require TSH recheck.
