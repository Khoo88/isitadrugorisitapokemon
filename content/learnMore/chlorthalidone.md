## What is Chlorthalidone?

Chlorthalidone is a thiazide-like diuretic that inhibits sodium-chloride cotransport in the distal convoluted tubule, increasing excretion of sodium, chloride, and water. It produces vasodilatory effects over time and reduces blood pressure effectively in hypertension trials, often with longer duration of action than hydrochlorothiazide.

Hypokalemia, hyponatremia, hyperuricemia, and hyperglycemia are class effects related to electrolyte and metabolic shifts. Volume depletion can cause orthostatic hypotension, especially in elderly patients.

It is a cornerstone agent in hypertension guidelines, frequently combined with renin-angiotensin blockers or calcium channel blockers.

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension (first-line thiazide-like agent in many guidelines)
- Edema associated with heart failure, renal disease, or hepatic disease (adjunct)
- Calcium nephrolithiasis prevention (reduces urinary calcium in selected hypercalciuric patients)
- Diabetes insipidus (paradoxical antidiuretic effect in nephrogenic DI at low doses)

## Common Side Effects

- Hypokalemia, hyponatremia
- Hyperuricemia (gout flares)
- Hyperglycemia
- Dizziness, orthostatic hypotension
- Muscle cramps
- Photosensitivity (less common than loop diuretics)
- Impaired renal function with volume depletion
- **Warnings:** Monitor electrolytes and renal function; use cautiously with QT-prolonging drugs when hypokalemia occurs; contraindicated in anuria; caution in gout and sulfa allergy (cross-reactivity uncommon but reported).
