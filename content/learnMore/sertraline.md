## What is Sertraline?

Sertraline is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI) that blocks the serotonin transporter (SERT), increasing synaptic serotonin in the central nervous system. It has mild dopamine reuptake inhibition at higher concentrations and minimal anticholinergic activity compared with tricyclic antidepressants.

Hepatic metabolism via CYP2C19 and CYP2D6 produces desmethylsertraline, an active metabolite. It is among the most prescribed antidepressants for depression, anxiety, and obsessive-compulsive disorder, with a relatively favorable side effect profile in many patients.

Onset of antidepressant effect requires 2–4 weeks; anxiety may improve earlier. Discontinuation syndrome can occur with abrupt stopping.

## Primary Uses

- Major depressive disorder
- Panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, generalized anxiety disorder
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder
- Post-traumatic stress disorder
- Premenstrual dysphoric disorder
- Premature ejaculation (off-label)

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, diarrhea, insomnia or somnolence
- Sexual dysfunction
- Headache, sweating
- Weight change
- Serotonin syndrome (with MAOIs or other serotonergic drugs)
- Bleeding risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants
- **Warnings:** Suicidality boxed warning in young adults—monitor early therapy; do not combine with MAOIs; taper on discontinuation; hyponatremia in elderly; QT prolongation uncommon at therapeutic doses.
