## What is Amlodipine?

Amlodipine is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker that selectively inhibits L-type calcium channels in vascular smooth muscle and cardiac tissue. Peripheral arteriolar vasodilation reduces systemic vascular resistance and blood pressure; coronary vasodilation improves myocardial oxygen supply.

Unlike non-dihydropyridines (verapamil, diltiazem), amlodipine has greater vascular selectivity and less negative chronotropic effect at usual doses. It has a long elimination half-life (30–50 hours), allowing once-daily dosing and smooth blood pressure control with less rebound than short-acting agents.

Hepatic metabolism via CYP3A4 produces minimal active metabolites. Grapefruit juice can increase plasma levels. Peripheral edema results from precapillary vasodilation and hydrostatic fluid transudation, a class effect often limiting titration.

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension (first-line per many guidelines, alone or combined)
- Chronic stable angina
- Vasospastic (Prinzmetal) angina
- Coronary artery disease risk reduction as part of comprehensive therapy

Often combined with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or thiazide diuretics when monotherapy is insufficient.

## Common Side Effects

- Peripheral edema (ankle swelling)
- Flushing, warmth
- Headache
- Dizziness, lightheadedness
- Palpitations (reflex tachycardia)
- Gingival hyperplasia (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Caution in severe aortic stenosis; do not withdraw abruptly in angina; use with caution in heart failure (some dihydropyridines worsen outcomes—amlodipine is among the better tolerated); hypotension in volume-depleted patients.
