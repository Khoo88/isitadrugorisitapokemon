## What is Clopidogrel?

Clopidogrel is a thienopyridine antiplatelet prodrug that irreversibly inhibits the P2Y12 adenosine diphosphate receptor on platelets, blocking platelet aggregation for the platelet lifespan (~7–10 days). Hepatic CYP2C19 activation converts clopidogrel to its active metabolite; poor metabolizers have reduced antiplatelet effect.

It is used with aspirin (dual antiplatelet therapy) after acute coronary syndromes and percutaneous coronary intervention, and alone or with aspirin in cerebrovascular and peripheral arterial disease per guidelines.

Bleeding risk increases with surgery, trauma, and concomitant anticoagulants or NSAIDs. Ticagrelor and prasugrel are alternative P2Y12 inhibitors with different potency and bleeding profiles.

## Primary Uses

- Acute coronary syndrome (with aspirin, post-stent)
- Recent myocardial infarction, ischemic stroke, or established peripheral arterial disease
- Post-coronary stent placement (dual antiplatelet therapy duration per stent type and bleeding risk)
- Atrial fibrillation when oral anticoagulation is not used (less common; guidelines favor anticoagulation for stroke prevention)

## Common Side Effects

- Bleeding (gastrointestinal, bruising, epistaxis)
- Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (rare)
- Rash, diarrhea
- Neutropenia (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Discontinuation before surgery per cardiologist guidance; proton pump inhibitors may reduce effect in some patients (clinical significance debated); CYP2C19 genotype or platelet function testing in selected high-risk cases; avoid with omeprazole/esomeprazole in some protocols.
