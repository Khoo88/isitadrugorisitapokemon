## What is Amoxicillin?

Amoxicillin is an aminopenicillin beta-lactam antibiotic that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), blocking peptidoglycan cross-linking and causing osmotic lysis of susceptible organisms. It is bactericidal against many gram-positive and some gram-negative aerobes.

Compared with penicillin G, amoxicillin has improved oral bioavailability and a broader gram-negative spectrum. It is often combined with clavulanate to overcome beta-lactamase production by resistant Haemophilus influenzae, Moraxella catarrhalis, and Staphylococcus aureus (non-MRSA).

Excretion is primarily renal; dose adjustment is needed in significant renal impairment. Hypersensitivity reactions cross-react with other beta-lactams in a percentage of penicillin-allergic patients, though true IgE-mediated allergy may allow cephalosporin use in selected cases after evaluation.

## Primary Uses

- Acute otitis media, sinusitis, streptococcal pharyngitis
- Community-acquired pneumonia (mild, outpatient)
- Dental abscess and odontogenic infections
- Helicobacter pylori eradication (as part of combination regimens)
- Lyme disease (early localized, per guidelines)
- Bacterial endocarditis prophylaxis (selected dental procedures in high-risk patients per current guidelines)
- Urinary tract infections (uncomplicated, when susceptible)

Amoxicillin-clavulanate extends coverage for beta-lactamase producers and bite wounds.

## Common Side Effects

- Diarrhea, nausea, abdominal discomfort
- Rash (non-allergic maculopapular rash, especially with EBV infection in mononucleosis)
- Allergic reactions (urticaria, anaphylaxis—rare but serious)
- Clostridioides difficile-associated colitis
- Hepatitis (especially with clavulanate combination, cholestatic pattern)
- Candidiasis (mucosal overgrowth with broad antibiotic use)
- **Warnings:** Assess penicillin allergy history; use weight-based pediatric dosing; adjust in renal failure; complete course per indication to reduce resistance.
