## What is Warfarin?

Warfarin is a vitamin K antagonist that inhibits hepatic synthesis of vitamin K–dependent clotting factors II, VII, IX, and X, as well as proteins C and S. Anticoagulation onset is delayed 24–72 hours until existing factors degrade; bridging with heparin is required for acute thrombosis treatment.

Narrow therapeutic index requires INR monitoring and dose adjustment for diet (vitamin K intake), medications, and illness. Genetic variants in CYP2C9 and VKORC1 affect dose requirements.

Despite DOAC availability, warfarin remains essential for mechanical heart valves, some LVAD patients, and when DOACs are contraindicated or unavailable.

## Primary Uses

- Atrial fibrillation stroke prevention
- Venous thromboembolism treatment and prevention
- Mechanical heart valve anticoagulation (target INR higher than AF)
- Post-myocardial infarction with large anterior wall motion abnormality (selected)
- Antiphospholipid syndrome (preferred over DOACs in many guidelines)

## Common Side Effects

- Bleeding (major and minor)
- Skin necrosis (rare, early protein C depletion)
- Purple toe syndrome (rare)
- **Warnings:** Extensive drug and food interactions; pregnancy teratogen (category X); INR monitoring; vitamin K reversal for serious bleeding; patient education on consistency of vitamin K–containing foods; fall risk in elderly.
