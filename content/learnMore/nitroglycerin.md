## What is Nitroglycerin?

Nitroglycerin is an organic nitrate that donates nitric oxide in vascular smooth muscle, activating guanylate cyclase and increasing cyclic GMP, causing venodilation and coronary artery dilation. Rapid sublingual absorption relieves acute angina within minutes by reducing preload and myocardial oxygen demand.

Transdermal patches and long-acting oral formulations prevent angina but require nitrate-free intervals (10–12 hours daily) to prevent tolerance. Headache is common and often tolerable with continued use.

Contraindicated with phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors (sildenafil, tadalafil, vardenafil) due to severe hypotension risk.

## Primary Uses

- Acute relief of angina pectoris (sublingual tablet or spray)
- Prophylaxis of angina (transdermal patch, oral extended-release with nitrate-free period)
- Hypertensive emergencies and acute heart failure (IV, specialized settings)
- Anal fissure (topical ointment, promotes sphincter relaxation)

Patients should carry sublingual nitroglycerin for chest pain protocols and seek emergency care if pain persists.

## Common Side Effects

- Headache (very common)
- Hypotension, dizziness, syncope
- Flushing
- Reflex tachycardia
- Tolerance with continuous exposure (avoid with proper dosing schedule)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated with PDE5 inhibitors; caution in right ventricular infarction and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with outflow obstruction; hypotension with alcohol; store sublingual tablets properly (potency loss with heat/moisture).
