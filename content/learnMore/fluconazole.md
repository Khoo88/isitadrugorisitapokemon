## What is Fluconazole?

Fluconazole is a triazole antifungal that inhibits fungal cytochrome P450-dependent enzyme lanosterol 14-alpha-demethylase, blocking ergosterol synthesis in the fungal cell membrane. It is fungistatic or fungicidal depending on organism and concentration.

Excellent oral bioavailability and cerebrospinal fluid penetration distinguish it from many azoles. Renal excretion of unchanged drug allows dosing in many patients without extensive hepatic metabolism, though dose reduction is required in renal impairment.

It has fewer drug interactions than itraconazole or ketoconazole but still inhibits CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 at higher doses, affecting warfarin and other substrates.

## Primary Uses

- Candidiasis (oral, esophageal, vaginal, systemic in selected patients)
- Cryptococcal meningitis (induction and maintenance with flucytosine/amphotericin per protocols)
- Coccidioidomycosis (maintenance therapy)
- Fungal prophylaxis in immunocompromised patients (selected settings)
- Onychomycosis (less effective than terbinafine for dermatophytes)

Empiric use should follow culture and susceptibility when possible due to resistance in Candida glabrata and C. krusei.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, abdominal pain
- Headache
- Rash
- Hepatotoxicity (elevated transaminases, rare fulminant hepatitis)
- QT prolongation (high doses or predisposed patients)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in pregnancy (teratogenic potential); major interaction with warfarin (INR monitoring); Stevens-Johnson syndrome (rare); adjust dose in renal failure.
