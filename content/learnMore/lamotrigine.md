## What is Lamotrigine?

Lamotrigine is an antiepileptic and mood-stabilizing drug that inhibits voltage-sensitive sodium channels, stabilizing neuronal membranes and inhibiting release of excitatory neurotransmitters such as glutamate. Exact mechanism in bipolar depression is not fully defined but involves glutamatergic modulation.

Slow titration is mandatory to reduce risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis, especially when combined with valproate (which inhibits lamotrigine glucuronidation, doubling levels). Oral contraceptives can lower lamotrigine levels, and stopping OCPs may raise levels—clinical monitoring is important in women of childbearing potential.

It is effective for bipolar depression maintenance with less weight gain than some alternatives but is not first-line for acute mania.

## Primary Uses

- Epilepsy (partial-onset and generalized seizures, adjunct or monotherapy)
- Bipolar disorder maintenance (prevention of depressive episodes)
- Lennox-Gastaut syndrome (adjunct, pediatric)
- Neuropathic pain (off-label, less common than gabapentinoids)

## Common Side Effects

- Rash (benign morbilliform to severe cutaneous adverse reactions)
- Dizziness, headache, diplopia
- Nausea, insomnia
- Ataxia
- **Warnings:** Titrate slowly per package insert; discontinue at first sign of serious rash; increased rash risk with valproate; pregnancy category risk (folate supplementation recommended); aseptic meningitis (rare).
