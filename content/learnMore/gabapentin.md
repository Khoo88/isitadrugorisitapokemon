## What is Gabapentin?

Gabapentin is a structural analog of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) that does not bind GABA receptors directly at clinically relevant sites. It binds the alpha-2-delta subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels, reducing excitatory neurotransmitter release and dampening neuronal hyperexcitability in neuropathic pain and seizure disorders.

Absorption is saturable at higher doses (bioavailability decreases as dose increases), requiring titration. Renal elimination is unchanged; dose adjustment is mandatory in renal impairment. Pregabalin is a related agent with more predictable pharmacokinetics.

Gabapentinoids are used in chronic pain syndromes; misuse and diversion occur, and some jurisdictions classify gabapentin as controlled.

## Primary Uses

- Postherpetic neuralgia
- Neuropathic pain associated with diabetic neuropathy (pregabalin often preferred; gabapentin still widely used)
- Adjunctive therapy for partial seizures
- Restless legs syndrome (off-label, per guidelines)
- Anxiety (off-label, variable evidence)

Not effective for most nociceptive pain as monotherapy.

## Common Side Effects

- Somnolence, dizziness, fatigue
- Ataxia, peripheral edema
- Weight gain (with long-term use)
- Mood changes, depression (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Do not discontinue abruptly in seizure patients; respiratory depression risk with opioids and CNS depressants; adjust dose in renal disease; impaired driving; substance misuse monitoring in some populations.
