## What is Orlistat?

Orlistat is a lipase inhibitor that binds gastric and pancreatic lipases in the gut lumen, preventing hydrolysis of dietary triglycerides into absorbable free fatty acids and monoglycerides. Approximately 30% of dietary fat passes unabsorbed, reducing caloric intake and supporting weight loss when combined with a reduced-calorie, low-fat diet.

Systemic absorption is minimal; effects are gastrointestinal and local. Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) may be depleted with chronic use; supplementation is recommended. OTC (60 mg) and prescription (120 mg) strengths exist.

Weight loss is modest (a few kilograms beyond diet alone on average) but may improve cardiometabolic risk factors in obese patients.

## Primary Uses

- Obesity management (BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with comorbidities) as adjunct to diet and exercise
- Weight maintenance after prior loss
- Reduction of type 2 diabetes risk in obese prediabetic patients (selected populations)

Not for cosmetic weight loss in normal-weight individuals.

## Common Side Effects

- Oily rectal discharge, flatus with discharge, fecal urgency
- Fatty or oily stools, increased defecation
- Abdominal cramping, fecal incontinence (worse with high-fat meals)
- Fat-soluble vitamin deficiency with prolonged use
- Rare: liver injury (hepatotoxicity warnings in labeling)
- **Warnings:** Take with meals containing fat; skip dose if meal has no fat; separate multivitamin by 2 hours at bedtime; may reduce absorption of cyclosporine and levothyroxine (separate dosing); cholestasis and chronic malabsorption are contraindications.
