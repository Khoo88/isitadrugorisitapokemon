## What is Lithium?

Lithium is a monovalent cation used as a mood stabilizer; its mechanism involves inhibition of inositol monophosphatase and glycogen synthase kinase-3, among other effects, altering intracellular signaling in neurons. It has a narrow therapeutic index: therapeutic serum levels (typically 0.6–1.2 mEq/L for maintenance) overlap with toxic levels.

Renal clearance is predominant; sodium depletion (diuretics, dehydration) raises levels and toxicity risk. It requires baseline and periodic monitoring of serum lithium, renal function, and thyroid function.

Toxicity presents with tremor, ataxia, confusion, seizures, and arrhythmias; overdose can be life-threatening without hemodialysis in severe cases.

## Primary Uses

- Bipolar I disorder (acute mania and maintenance)
- Bipolar depression maintenance (often with antidepressants)
- Augmentation of antidepressants in treatment-resistant depression (selected patients)
- Cluster headache prophylaxis (off-label)

Gold standard mood stabilizer with strongest evidence for suicide prevention in bipolar disorder.

## Common Side Effects

- Fine tremor, polyuria, polydipsia (nephrogenic diabetes insipidus)
- Weight gain
- Cognitive dulling
- Nausea, diarrhea (often transient)
- Hypothyroidism, hyperparathyroidism
- Renal insufficiency with long-term use
- Acne, psoriasis exacerbation
- **Warnings:** Narrow therapeutic window; toxicity with dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, diuretics; pregnancy category D (Ebstein anomaly risk); maintain adequate salt and fluid intake; levels 12 hours post-dose.
