## What is Quetiapine?

Quetiapine is an atypical antipsychotic with antagonism at dopamine D2, serotonin 5-HT2A, histamine H1, and adrenergic alpha-1 receptors. Low therapeutic doses used in insomnia and anxiety rely heavily on sedating H1 blockade; antipsychotic doses provide D2 antagonism for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder.

Extended-release formulation allows once-daily dosing. Metabolism via CYP3A4 produces norquetiapine, an active metabolite with norepinephrine reuptake inhibition properties.

Metabolic adverse effects (weight gain, dyslipidemia, hyperglycemia) are significant at antipsychotic doses; sedation and orthostatic hypotension are common.

## Primary Uses

- Schizophrenia (acute and maintenance)
- Bipolar I disorder (mania, depression, maintenance)
- Major depressive disorder (adjunctive therapy at lower doses)
- Generalized anxiety disorder (off-label, low dose—limited evidence)
- Insomnia (off-label, low dose—not preferred first-line)

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, somnolence
- Orthostatic hypotension, dizziness
- Weight gain, metabolic syndrome
- Dry mouth, constipation
- Extrapyramidal symptoms (lower than typical antipsychotics at low doses)
- QT prolongation (dose-related)
- **Warnings:** Increased mortality in elderly with dementia-related psychosis (boxed warning); tardive dyskinesia with chronic use; monitor metabolic parameters; neuroleptic malignant syndrome (rare).
