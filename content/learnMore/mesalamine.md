## What is Mesalamine?

Mesalamine (5-aminosalicylic acid, 5-ASA) is an anti-inflammatory agent that acts topically on colonic mucosa when delivered via delayed-release oral formulations, enemas, or suppositories. Exact mechanisms include inhibition of leukotriene and prostaglandin synthesis, scavenging reactive oxygen species, and modulating mucosal immune responses.

Sulfasalazine is a prodrug cleaved by colonic bacteria to mesalamine and sulfapyridine; mesalamine formulations avoid sulfapyridine side effects (headache, hemolysis in G6PD deficiency). Formulations target ileum and colon differently (pH-dependent, multimatrix, etc.).

Systemic absorption is low with most topical delivery, but monitoring is still relevant in renal impairment.

## Primary Uses

- Ulcerative colitis (induction and maintenance of remission)
- Crohn’s disease involving colon (mild to moderate, maintenance)
- Proctitis and distal colitis (rectal formulations)
- Prevention of post-polypectomy recurrence in some protocols (specialist)

Not effective for small-bowel Crohn’s without colonic involvement.

## Common Side Effects

- Headache
- Nausea, abdominal pain, flatulence
- Diarrhea
- Rash
- Nephrotoxicity (interstitial nephritis, rare—monitor renal function)
- Pancreatitis, pericarditis (rare hypersensitivity)
- **Warnings:** Mesalamine-induced acute intolerance may mimic flare (worsening diarrhea, cramping); check renal function periodically; sulfasalazine carries separate sulfa toxicity profile.
