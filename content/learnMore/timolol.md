## What is Timolol?

Timolol is a non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker used primarily as topical ophthalmic solution or gel to lower intraocular pressure in glaucoma by reducing aqueous humor production by the ciliary body. Systemic absorption occurs and can cause bradycardia, bronchospasm, and masked hypoglycemia in susceptible patients.

Oral timolol is used for hypertension and migraine prophylaxis but ophthalmic use is the most common exposure. Once- or twice-daily dosing depends on formulation.

Beta-blockade in the eye does not provide the visual field benefits of prostaglandin analogs but remains effective and inexpensive first-line therapy in many settings.

## Primary Uses

- Open-angle glaucoma
- Ocular hypertension
- Migraine prophylaxis (oral)
- Hypertension (oral, less common today)

## Common Side Effects

**Ophthalmic:**
- Ocular burning, stinging
- Bradycardia, hypotension, bronchospasm (systemic absorption)
- Fatigue, depression (systemic effects)

**Oral:**
- Bradycardia, fatigue, bronchospasm
- **Warnings:** Caution in asthma and COPD; use punctal occlusion to reduce systemic absorption; avoid oral nonselective beta-blocker duplication; may worsen heart block; mask hypoglycemia in diabetes.
