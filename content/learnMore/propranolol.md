## What is Propranolol?

Propranolol is a non-selective beta-adrenergic blocker antagonizing beta-1 and beta-2 receptors, reducing heart rate, contractility, and renin release while causing bronchospasm risk in reactive airways. It is lipophilic, crosses the blood-brain barrier, and is useful for migraine prophylaxis and essential tremor in addition to cardiovascular indications.

Hepatic metabolism via CYP2D6 produces active and inactive metabolites. Non-selective blockade prevents beta-2–mediated bronchodilation and glycogenolysis, relevant in asthma and insulin-treated diabetes.

Immediate-release and long-acting formulations support hypertension, angina, and performance anxiety (off-label situational use).

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension
- Angina pectoris
- Post-myocardial infarction (secondary prevention)
- Atrial fibrillation and supraventricular tachycardia rate control
- Migraine prophylaxis
- Essential tremor
- Thyrotoxicosis and thyroid storm (adjunct)
- Infantile hemangioma (oral solution, specialized pediatric use)
- Portal hypertension (variceal bleeding prophylaxis, with nitrates)

## Common Side Effects

- Bradycardia, hypotension
- Fatigue, dizziness
- Bronchospasm (asthma, COPD)
- Cold extremities
- Sleep disturbance, vivid dreams
- Masked hypoglycemia symptoms
- **Warnings:** Do not stop abruptly in ischemic heart disease; contraindicated in decompensated heart failure and high-grade AV block; caution in diabetes; may worsen Raynaud phenomenon.
