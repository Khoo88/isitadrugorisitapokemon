## What is Diazepam?

Diazepam is a long-acting benzodiazepine that potentiates GABA at GABAA receptors, producing anxiolytic, sedative, muscle relaxant, and anticonvulsant effects. It has a long half-life and active metabolites (desmethyldiazepam), leading to prolonged drug effect and accumulation with repeated dosing.

Oral, rectal, intravenous, and intramuscular routes support use in acute seizure termination, alcohol withdrawal, muscle spasm, and procedural sedation. Lipid solubility allows rapid central nervous system penetration.

Schedule IV controlled substance with significant dependence, misuse, and withdrawal risks including seizures if tapered abruptly after chronic use.

## Primary Uses

- Acute alcohol withdrawal syndrome (symptom-triggered or fixed taper protocols)
- Status epilepticus and acute repetitive seizures (rectal gel, IV)
- Muscle spasm (short-term)
- Anxiety disorders (short-term; not first-line chronic therapy)
- Procedural sedation and premedication (IV, per anesthesia protocols)
- Nightmares and PTSD-related sleep disturbance (off-label, limited)

## Common Side Effects

- Sedation, confusion, amnesia
- Ataxia, falls (elderly)
- Respiratory depression (especially with opioids)
- Dependence and withdrawal
- Paradoxical agitation (rare)
- **Warnings:** Boxed warning with opioids; avoid abrupt discontinuation; prolonged IV use and preservatives linked to propylene glycol toxicity in ICU settings; pregnancy risk; impaired driving.
