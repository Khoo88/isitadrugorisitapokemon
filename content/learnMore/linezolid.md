## What is Linezolid?

Linezolid is an oxazolidinone antibiotic that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis by binding the 23S ribosomal RNA of the 50S subunit, preventing formation of the initiation complex. It is bacteriostatic against most gram-positive organisms, including methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) and vancomycin-resistant enterococci (VRE).

Oral and intravenous formulations have 100% bioavailability equivalence. Weak, reversible monoamine oxidase inhibition can cause serotonin syndrome with serotonergic drugs and pressor responses with sympathomimetics.

Thrombocytopenia and peripheral/optic neuropathy occur with prolonged courses (>2 weeks), requiring monitoring.

## Primary Uses

- Complicated skin and soft tissue infections due to resistant gram-positive organisms
- Nosocomial pneumonia due to MRSA
- VRE infections (including bloodstream, when susceptible)
- Other serious gram-positive infections when alternatives are limited

Reserved for resistant infections or beta-lactam allergy in serious disease—not empiric first-line for uncomplicated infections.

## Common Side Effects

- Nausea, diarrhea, headache
- Thrombocytopenia (especially >2 weeks)
- Peripheral and optic neuropathy (prolonged use)
- Serotonin syndrome (with SSRIs, SNRIs, meperidine, etc.)
- Lactic acidosis (rare)
- **Warnings:** Avoid or use extreme caution with serotonergic antidepressants; monitor CBC weekly in prolonged therapy; ophthalmologic monitoring for therapy >3 months; reversible myelosuppression.
