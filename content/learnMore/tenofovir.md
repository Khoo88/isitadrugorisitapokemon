## What is Tenofovir?

Tenofovir refers to prodrug formulations of tenofovir used in HIV and hepatitis B therapy: tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF) and tenofovir alafenamide (TAF). Both are converted intracellularly to tenofovir diphosphate, which inhibits viral reverse transcriptase and HBV polymerase by chain termination.

TAF delivers higher intracellular active drug with lower plasma tenofovir exposure, reducing renal and bone toxicity compared with TDF at standard doses. TDF remains widely used in generic combination tablets.

Part of single-tablet regimens (e.g., with emtricitabine and dolutegravir or elvitegravir) for HIV and dual therapy for HBV.

## Primary Uses

- HIV-1 infection (as part of combination antiretroviral therapy)
- Chronic hepatitis B infection
- HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (tenofovir/emtricitabine—Truvada or Descovy formulations)
- Post-exposure prophylaxis (per CDC)

Always combined for HIV except in research contexts; HBV monotherapy can select resistance if used alone in HIV-coinfected patients without full HIV regimen.

## Common Side Effects

**TDF:**
- Renal tubular dysfunction, decreased bone mineral density
- Nausea, headache

**TAF:**
- Generally fewer renal/bone effects
- Weight gain (class observation with some integrase combinations)

- **Warnings:** Monitor renal function and bone density with TDF; test for HBV before stopping (flare risk); lactic acidosis/hepatomegaly (rare, class historical); avoid TDF with nephrotoxic agents when possible.
