## What is Omeprazole?

Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) that irreversibly inhibits the H+/K+-ATPase enzyme on gastric parietal cell canaliculi, blocking the final common pathway of gastric acid secretion. Acid suppression is profound and longer-lasting than H2 antagonists because new pump molecules must be synthesized to restore acid production.

It is a prodrug activated in acidic parietal cell canals. CYP2C19 metabolism affects exposure; poor metabolizers may have greater acid suppression. Long-term use is associated with hypomagnesemia, B12 deficiency, fracture risk debate, and increased enteric infection risk (C. difficile, some enteric organisms).

Available OTC and by prescription; OTC duration should be limited without medical evaluation.

## Primary Uses

- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (erosive and nonerosive)
- Peptic ulcer disease (gastric and duodenal, including H. pylori triple therapy component)
- Zollinger-Ellison syndrome
- Erosive esophagitis healing and maintenance
- NSAID-associated ulcer prophylaxis
- Dyspepsia (short-term OTC)

## Common Side Effects

- Headache
- Nausea, diarrhea, abdominal pain
- Flatulence
- Long-term: hypomagnesemia, vitamin B12 deficiency, kidney disease association (acute interstitial nephritis—rare)
- Increased risk of C. difficile infection
- **Warnings:** May reduce absorption of drugs requiring acid (ketoconazole, iron); CYP2C19 interaction affects clopidogrel active metabolite (consider alternative PPI or agent in acute coronary syndrome per cardiology guidance); evaluate alarm symptoms before chronic OTC use.
