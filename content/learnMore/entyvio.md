## What is Entyvio?

Entyvio (vedolizumab) is a humanized monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the α4β7 integrin on memory T lymphocytes, blocking interaction with mucosal addressin cell adhesion molecule-1 (MAdCAM-1) on intestinal endothelium. This inhibits lymphocyte trafficking into gut mucosa, reducing intestinal inflammation in inflammatory bowel disease.

Unlike systemic anti-TNF agents, vedolizumab has gut-selective immunomodulation with lower systemic immunosuppression in theory, though serious infections still occur. It is administered by intravenous infusion with optional subcutaneous maintenance dosing after induction.

Response may take several weeks; induction typically involves infusions at weeks 0, 2, and 6.

## Primary Uses

- Moderate to severe ulcerative colitis in adults inadequately responding to corticosteroids, immunomodulators, or anti-TNF therapy
- Moderate to severe Crohn’s disease in adults with similar treatment history

Used when biologic therapy is indicated and gut-selective mechanism is preferred; not for sepsis or acute severe colitis without concurrent steroids and surgical planning.

## Common Side Effects

- Nasopharyngitis, upper respiratory infections
- Headache, arthralgia
- Infusion-related reactions (urticaria, nausea)
- Increased risk of serious infections (less than systemic immunosuppressants in some comparisons but still present)
- Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy risk (theoretical; lower than natalizumab without JC virus strategy)
- Liver enzyme elevations (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Screen for tuberculosis and hepatitis B; avoid live vaccines; discontinue if serious infection; hepatotoxicity monitoring per labeling.
