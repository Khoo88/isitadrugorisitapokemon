## What is Finasteride?

Finasteride is a synthetic 4-azasteroid that selectively inhibits 5-alpha-reductase type II, the enzyme that converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) in the prostate and scalp. Reduced DHT shrinks benign prostatic tissue over months and slows androgenetic hair loss in men.

At standard doses for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), serum DHT falls substantially while testosterone may rise slightly. A higher dose is used for male pattern hair loss. Finasteride is teratogenic—pregnant women must not handle crushed tablets due to risk of abnormal fetal genital development.

The related agent dutasteride inhibits both type I and type II 5-alpha-reductase with broader DHT suppression. Effects on prostate volume and symptoms are gradual, requiring months of therapy.

## Primary Uses

- Benign prostatic hyperplasia (symptomatic)—reduces prostate volume, improves urinary flow and symptoms
- Male androgenetic alopecia (pattern hair loss) at lower daily dose
- Off-label uses (e.g., hirsutism in women) are uncommon due to teratogenicity and endocrine effects

Often combined with alpha-blockers (tamsulosin) for faster symptomatic relief in BPH while finasteride provides long-term size reduction.

## Common Side Effects

- Decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, ejaculatory disorders
- Gynecomastia (uncommon)
- Orthostatic symptoms (when combined with alpha-blockers)
- Mood changes and depression (reported, under active study)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated in pregnancy; women of childbearing potential should avoid exposure; may affect PSA levels (inform clinicians for cancer screening interpretation); rare reports of persistent sexual side effects after discontinuation.
