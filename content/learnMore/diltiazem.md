## What is Diltiazem?

Diltiazem is a non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker with predominant cardiac effects: it slows sinoatrial and atrioventricular nodal conduction and reduces myocardial contractility at higher concentrations, while causing coronary and peripheral vasodilation.

Immediate-release, extended-release, and intravenous formulations support hypertension, angina, and rate control in atrial fibrillation/flutter. Metabolism via CYP3A4 creates drug interaction potential with statins, cyclosporine, and other substrates.

Unlike dihydropyridines (amlodipine), diltiazem is useful when heart rate control is therapeutic goal but contraindicated in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and high-grade AV block without pacemaker.

## Primary Uses

- Hypertension
- Chronic stable angina and vasospastic angina
- Atrial fibrillation/flutter rate control (acute IV and oral maintenance)
- Supraventricular tachycardia (acute IV, per ACLS-adjacent protocols)

Often combined cautiously with beta-blockers for rate control (additive bradycardia risk).

## Common Side Effects

- Bradycardia, heart block
- Hypotension
- Peripheral edema (less than dihydropyridines)
- Constipation, dyspepsia
- Headache, dizziness
- Elevated liver enzymes (uncommon)
- **Warnings:** Avoid in sick sinus syndrome without pacemaker, second- or third-degree AV block, acute decompensated heart failure, and WPW with atrial fibrillation; grapefruit increases levels; taper not usually required but monitor blood pressure.
