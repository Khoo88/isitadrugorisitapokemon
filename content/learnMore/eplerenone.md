## What is Eplerenone?

Eplerenone is a selective aldosterone receptor antagonist (mineralocorticoid receptor blocker) that competes with aldosterone in the kidney and heart, promoting sodium excretion and potassium retention while reducing fibrotic signaling. Unlike spironolactone, it has minimal affinity for androgen and progesterone receptors, reducing gynecomastia and sexual side effects.

It is indicated post-myocardial infarction with heart failure and in systolic heart failure, and for hypertension. Monitoring serum potassium and renal function is mandatory due to hyperkalemia risk, especially with ACE inhibitors, ARBs, or renal impairment.

Metabolism via CYP3A4 requires avoidance of strong inhibitors (ketoconazole, clarithromycin).

## Primary Uses

- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction post-MI (LVEF ≤40%, with heart failure or diabetes)
- Chronic heart failure (NYHA class II–IV, LVEF ≤40%)
- Hypertension (adjunct or alternative when aldosterone excess suspected)

Often added to ACE/ARB and beta-blocker therapy when patients remain symptomatic or after MI per guidelines.

## Common Side Effects

- Hyperkalemia
- Dizziness, hypotension
- Diarrhea
- Cough (less than ACE inhibitors)
- Gynecomastia (rare, less than spironolactone)
- **Warnings:** Contraindicated if potassium >5.0 mEq/L, creatinine clearance <30 mL/min (heart failure dosing), or on strong CYP3A4 inhibitors; monitor potassium and creatinine within 3–7 days of initiation and after dose changes; avoid potassium supplements and salt substitutes.
